## 3. Built form
Colonial: timber and brick houses with verandas and tin roofs (the museum is a prefabricated 1884 governor's house). Contemporary: block bungalows and storey buildings, plazas, a harbour with warehouses, a tree-covered city between two rivers. **Thin**: no street-level source.

## 4. Road
Asphalt with roundabouts (Orok Orok), a river port, a harbour road, rain-slick wet roads. Pool taxis and keke (okada banned since 2009); no scheduled buses; keke protested ticket fees in March 2026 and a 2013 report said keke had once been kept to the suburbs (no current rule found). The draft ride was danfo; the approved ride is keke.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
Humid rainforest city: raffia, oil palm, rubber, cocoyam and plantain, mangroves along the estuary, a wide river with ferries, hills inland. Rain nine months a year, steady warm temperature, haze on the water.

## 6. Dress
Everyday city dress; Efik women's wrapper (usobo, etenge) and blouse. Ukara cloth and nsibidi motifs belong to the Ekpe society, a secret society, so they are avoided. Carnival costumes are band-coloured and use party-adjacent colours (red, green, orange, blue, yellow): avoid as full outfits.

## 7. Signs and language
English and Pidgin on boards, Efik and Ejagham in the state. Board words: CARNIVAL, MARKET, FISH, POS, TRADE. An Efik line needs a native speaker.
