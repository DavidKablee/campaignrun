## 3. Built form
Osogbo: a trading city of block shops and plazas, a ring of dual carriageways and interchanges. A study of 406 households (Jiboye 2014, Frontiers of Architectural Research 3(1)) found 80% living in "face-me-I-face-you" rooming houses (a long block with a corridor and a row of doors), 14.8% in Western-style apartments and 1.2% in traditional courtyard houses; a second study lists traditional houses, rooming apartments, apartments, bungalows and duplexes. So the lot kit leads with long single-storey rooming blocks with a shared veranda and zinc roofs. **Thin**: no street-level source; reuse the Yoruba city kit (Ogun, Oyo) with a dye-trade twist. Dye yards: open ground with round clay-rimmed pits, cloth hung on lines to dry, blue-black and white.

## 4. Road
Dual carriageways and a ring road with interchanges, the Western rail line crossing the city, patched asphalt, laterite verges. Okada and keke and buses (**thin**: no colour or rule source). The Osun River is a murky brown (reports of contamination: do not draw as a toxic scene, plain muddy water).

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
Forest-savanna mosaic: tall trees mixed with grass, cocoa and cashew farms, cassava and yam plots, oil palm, banana. A river with stream tributaries. Wet season roughly April–October, harmattan haze December–February (state page gives conflicting figures; avoid numbers).

## 6. Dress
Yoruba city dress: buba and iro, gele, men's buba and sokoto or shirts, caps (fila). Osogbo-specific mark: indigo adire cloth (blue-black with white resist patterns) as wrapper, shawl or head tie. **Thin** for everyday dress (the Lagos study says adire is fading from daily wear). Avoid: white-robed religious dress, beaded regalia, an Ooni's or Oba's crown.

## 7. Signs and language
