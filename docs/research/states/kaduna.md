## 3. Built form
**What Kaduna is:** a planned colonial capital founded c. 1913, made capital of the Northern Protectorate in 1917, laid out on a railway junction in open country with large plots, gardens and trees and "carefully planned" road layouts [2+: S6, S45 and a Daily Trust history summary]. Unlike Kano it has no ancient walled core; the Hausa walled city is at Zaria, 80 km north.

**Colonial / Government Reservation Area (GRA) quarter [S6 unless noted]**
- Big plots, houses set in gardens and trees, spacious compounds, large verandas, deep overhanging eaves [1, S6 plus a second paper summary].
- Victorian-derived public and residential buildings: deep-pitched roofs, deep porticos; civic buildings with arches, cornices, quoins, balustrades, pediments (the central prison front and state secretariat are the named examples) [1].
- The colonial legislature hall: a dome with a pinnacle ("zanko") added by 1956, mixing Hausa dome/pinnacle with neoclassical elements; Britannica calls it "simplified Islamic style" and sits at the head of the main street within a circular road [2+ for dome; pinnacle 1].
- **Not verified:** roof sheeting. The brief's "red-tile or zinc hipped roofs" is plausible but no Kaduna source states it for colonial bungalows. Verified for later housing: two-storey middle-class houses with deep multi-level **tiled** roofs, coloured external tiles, arches, balconies, narrow windows on the north side of the city; beams-and-columns, glass balusters, wider windows and mild exterior colours on the south side [1, S6].
- Wall colour not stated; limewash cream/white with a darker eaves line is an [inf] default.

**Post-independence and contemporary city [S6 unless noted]**
- 1970s: rectangular masses, sandcrete block with cement-sand render, wide glass openings, emulsion paint, concrete fins, flat roofs; office blocks by trading companies on the main avenue; a ten-storey government office block (1975) [1].
- 1980s-90s: concrete facades, parapets, screen walls, sculptured concrete, window hoods, buttresses [1].
- 2000s onward: glass walls, "interlocking cubic forms", plane walls, capping/mould details, narrow windows, **high-pitched tiled roofs**, hard paving, membrane structures [1].
- Commercial: plazas on the main avenues; the biggest has an underground floor plus a four-storey block [1, S2; an AI-generated listing, treat cautiously]. Bank Road is a retail/office corridor [1, search listing].
- Interchanges: a roundabout converted to flyover plus underpass; the Kawo flyover has a curved (parabolic) profile and is being rebuilt with raised shorter piers [2+: S6, S19]. Security-grill ironwork (burglar-proof window frames, rolling garage doors, metal gates) is a daily product of the Panteka workshops [1: S12, Commons filenames only] and should appear on every shopfront and gate.
- Two-sided city: bars and mixed neighbourhoods more in the south, conservative decorative style in the north [1, S2, S6].

**Vernacular Hausa edge (Zaria, Tudun Wada fringes, old villages)**
- Tubali sun-dried mud brick in cubic blocks; parapets; plastered white or earth-coloured fronts with relief patterns, sometimes painted in vivid colours; multi-storey elite houses [2+: S27, S28].
- Compound entered through a zaure (entry hut/reception room) with exposed azara palm beams [2+: S26 and search summaries].
- Roofs are flat or vaulted in the old city; the museum's "mud and thatch" Hausa-style buildings [1, S40, promotional text]. Flat parapeted roofs are [inf] for Zaria compounds (not stated in a Kaduna source).
- A Commons file "Mud house Kaduna 1960" shows mud houses existed inside the city [1].

**Southern Kaduna (Kafanchan, Kagoro, Zangon Kataf, Kachia, Chikun/Gbagyi edge)**
- Katab/Atyap houses: stone plastered with mud, thatch roof with a "jutting peak in the front"; older villages compact and walled, later spread out; compound of about 7 people [1, S29]. A personal village essay (search summary) describes rows of long mud huts facing each other inside a square shared compound [1]. A travel account describes thatched huts dotted across open land among maize, guinea corn and ginger [1].
- **Round huts are not confirmed.** Use a rectangular-to-oblong mud-plastered hut with a steep thatched gable and a front overhang for the village strip; a round hut with cone thatch is a plausible alternative but unverified. Most towns (Kafanchan, Kagoro) are now block-and-zinc [inf]; keep thatch to roughly a third of lots in village strips only.
- Terrain frames the built form: granite boulders and rocky defile near Zangon Kataf [2+: S32 and Barth quote], Kagoro massif [2+].

**Industrial and rail**
- Kakuri/Makera ("Textile Road"): mill compounds behind **very long blank walls**, gates still standing, overgrown yards, derelict sheds; small kiosks of zinc and wood against the walls; some new plants (tractor assembly, poultry feed mill) [1, S20; textile mills opened 1957 and closed by 2002 per S21]. Saw-tooth mill roofs are [inf]; do not state as fact.
- Refinery in the city (tall stacks and tanks) [1, S1]. Inland dry port [1, S1].
- Rail: Cape-gauge junction; a narrow-gauge **through-truss** bridge over the River Kaduna dated 1911 by a Commons caption but 1927 by a history summary (date conflict [2+ on existence]); level crossing at Kawo; modern standard-gauge station at Rigasa; a smaller station at Tsaunin Kura. The old station's platforms, canopy and signal gantries could not be verified; propose in section 8/12 as an [inf] generic colonial pattern (long low brick/rendered building, deep verandah canopy on iron posts, hanging name board, semaphore or lamp mast).

## 4. Road
- Main city roads are asphalt, "well paved" and busy; several are dual carriageways with medians; roundabouts are numerous and some carry monuments (football on a cup-like pedestal at the township stadium roundabout) [2+: S22, S19, S10].
- State programme: 19 road projects, 12 dualised, solar streetlights on all, flyovers (Arewa-area junction, Kawo, Leventis), an underpass, a new bridge over the River Kaduna and an unnamed bridge on the western bypass [1, S19]. Night lighting makes roundabouts glow brighter than the day [1, S22].
- **Kerbs, markings, poles, wires, signs: no source.** [inf] for modelling: low concrete kerbs on the dual avenues and in the GRA, none on side streets; shoulder of red-brown laterite that blows as dust; concrete utility poles with bundled service wires; green or blue direction signs in English; painted zebra bars only at big junctions.
- Verges: GRA avenues have deep verges with large trees (inferred from the plot/garden planning in S6; the width is not measured).
- Level crossing: at Kawo a road crosses the railway; trucks have queued there [1, S44]. Model as a lowered boom or painted cross-buck plus rails flush in tarmac; do not depict a collision.
- Roadside trade: tea-sellers' tents (mai shayi) and noodle (indomie) stalls with log or bench seats; fruit sellers around the stadium roundabout; used-clothing and produce sellers near markets; hawkers [2+: S22, S2, S11].
- Vehicles: keke (NAPEP) tricycles dominant, okada (motorcycle taxis) present but restricted on some corridors, scarce taxis and minibuses, multicoloured patched-paint buses [2+: S22, S24]. Keke body colour in Kaduna is **not verified**.
- Bridges seen from the road: iron/steel truss rail bridge, an older motor-road bridge, a modern road bridge under construction/commissioning [1 each].

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- Savanna woodland: northern Guinea savanna with Sudan elements. Characteristic trees: Isoberlinia doka (dominant), Parkia biglobosa (locust bean, Hausa dorowa/ dorawa [inf spelling]), Vitellaria paradoxa (shea), Daniellia oliveri, Terminalia avicennioides, Prosopis africana [2+: S34, S3, S30]. Neem is called "dogon yaro" in Hausa [1, S39] and is a standard street tree [inf]. Mango (red mangoes in markets) [1, S11]. Silk-cotton tree is large and culturally important near Zaria [1, S28]. Teak, eucalyptus and gmelina are planted (tree-planting distributions) [1, search summary]. Cashew, guava, pawpaw, banana, oil palm in the south [1, S29].
- Crops: groundnut, cotton, sorghum ("guinea corn"), maize; ginger in the south (state is a leading ginger area); millet, fonio, yam, cassava, beniseed in southern villages [2+: S3, S29, S30, S32].
- Terrain: flat plain broken by granite outcrops (Zaria rocky outcrops; Kufena Hills reported at ~802-825 m, a National Monument since 1956 [1, S51; elevation conflict]); Kajuru hilltop castle in granite; Kagoro massif (reported 1,152 m / ~2,000 ft [2+, conflicting numbers]); Matsirga falls 25-30 m into a gorge, fed by springs on Kagoro Hills, strongest June-September [2+: S33, S3]. Soils loamy to sandy with clay [1, S3]; sandstone-derived around Zangon Kataf [1, S30]. **Red laterite** colour is [inf]: no fetched source says "laterite"; red-brown is still the safest ground tone for this latitude.
- Water: the Kaduna River rises on the Jos plateau, flows ~500 km to the Niger, supplies the city [1, S4]. Many communities flood seasonally in the rains [1, S3].
- Climate (Aw): dry season Nov-Mar, zero rain Nov-Jan; rain peaks August (~335 mm), April-May hottest (max ~36 C); nights 15-18 C in the dry season [2+: S1, S2, S4].
- Light by season:
  - Dry/harmattan (Dec-Feb): cool nights, hot days, dust haze with visibility from 1,000 m to 5,000 m, sun a pale disc, trees thin and leaves dull, grass yellow-to-brown [2+: S35, S22, S30].
  - Late dry (Mar-Apr): heat shimmer, bare ground, bush fires smoke [inf].
  - Rains (Jun-Sep): fields and trees green, heavy cumulus, high humidity and cloud [2+: S3, S30].
- Compared with Kano: more and bigger trees, visible hills, a river, and a greener/cooler sky; Kano's scenery is dye pits, baobab/date palm and a near-treeless plain.

## 6. Dress
**Men (northern/Hausa) [2+: S27, S22]:** babban riga (wide, side-open gown with chest embroidery) over trousers; tagguwa/short riga; plain kaftan; tee shirt and trousers or jeans for work and youth; embroidered hula cap or a wrapped turban; leather sandals and slippers. Colours: white, cream, sky blue, grey-blue, sand, olive, brown, faded indigo.
**Women (northern) [2+: S27, S49]:** zani wrapper of atampa/Ankara print, matching blouse, headtie (kallabi) and shawl (gyale); various veils and hijab styles; henna on hands [1, S27].
**Children [inf]:** plain school uniform shirts in blue-and-white or khaki-and-white without logos; small kaftans; flip-flops. No source checked.
**Southern communities:** everyday dress is shared with the rest of the state (Ankara-print wrapper and blouse, headtie, shirt and trousers) [inf]. Gbagyi: dark-blue cloth worn as trousers/skirts with white shirts [1, S31]; a black-and-white print cloth reading "we are Gbagyi people" exists [1, S31]. Older Atyap dress (leather aprons, raffia, cowries, beaded edges, chalk and ochre body painting) is historical and festival wear, **not everyday** [1, S29]. Do not dress southern NPCs in historic or "tribal" costume.
**Working dress:** welders and ironworkers in overalls/aprons, face shields or goggles [inf from S12 filenames]; market traders in wrappers and caps; mai shayi in apron and cap; tractor-plant and rail workers in hi-vis vest and hard hat [inf].
**Footwear:** sandals, slippers, canvas shoes; no uniform boots.
**Avoid list**
- Solid red caps, red-and-white or red-green-white cap and kaftan combinations, solid green or solid blue kaftan crowds, and any cap, scarf or sash colour block that reads as a party or movement identity. Atyap red caps are a real heritage item [1, S30] but must not be used.
- A single cap shape in one saturated colour repeated across a crowd, "fila"-style political caps, an NPC in a full matching riga, cap and dark glasses ("politician look"; S39 notes the babban riga is associated with politicians) with a staff or fan.
- Military or police uniforms, camouflage, weapons, masked faces, kidnap or checkpoint imagery; Kaduna hosts an army academy and a garrison, so uniformed figures must not appear. No burning, riot or barricade imagery.
- Herder-coded imagery: staff-carrying herdsmen, cattle drives, gourds and bags slung with a herd, Fulani-coded costume as a crowd element. Donkeys and goats at market are fine; cattle are not.
- Ethnic-stereotype imagery: loincloth or raffia "tribal" figures, face marks as decoration, thatched hut as the only picture of southern Kaduna, "primitive" framing.
- Religious dress as a marker (clerical robes, church-hat or mosque-hat as a group signal) and prayer scenes.

## 9. Rejected candidates
- Colonial legislature hall (named for the first governor): person-named; a legislature building; also used by politicians.
- Colonial iron footbridge (named for the first governor), a roundabout and a road with the same name: person-named. The footbridge's shape may still appear as an unnamed park prop.
- Main stadium (named for a regional premier), central square (named for a former head of state), park formerly named for a general, main avenue and many roads (named for premiers/heads of state), central market's current official name, the historic regional-party building: person-named or politician-linked.
- Plazas named for politicians' surnames (e.g. the "biggest plaza"): person-named, AI-written listing.
- Leventis Roundabout/Underpass: named for a company founder's surname; reject unless the reviewer says a company name is fine.
- "Gamji Gate" / "Kofar Gamji": "Gamji" is both a tree and the nickname of a regional political figure [1, S48]; reject the name even though the gate is an old landmark.
- Mosques (the large central mosque), cathedrals and churches: religious.
- Emir's palace, Zaria (and the chief's palaces at Zonkwa, Kagoro): palace = scenery only.
- Kajuru Castle as a venue: private resort; 2019 armed attack and kidnapping; insecurity link. Scenery only if the reviewer wants it.
- Rigasa Railway Station: 2022 attack on a train; commissioned by a former head of state. Rail-yard scenery stays; this station does not.
- Kangimi Dam: 2026 structural-crack alarm and flood warnings, same logic as the Alau decision in C27.
- Kamuku National Park: wildlife park, but the Birnin Gwari side is associated with insecurity; also herd/herder imagery risk.
- Existing pack names not found in any source: **Centenary Park, Rigasa Square, Central Stadium Ground**. A "Rigasa Square" may exist but the only Rigasa items found were a railway station and an erosion photo; recommend replacing all three. Existing Kawo Market was demolished in the redevelopment (S16).
- Panteka Market, Kasuwar Barci: market fire(s) and demolition disputes; use the trades, not the names.
- Kaduna Golf Club, Polo Club clubhouse: private clubs; single-line sources.

## 11. Hazard visual notes
- **dust-plume:** low tan-pink laterite dust cloud behind trucks and from unpaved shoulders and building yards; 4-6 stacked translucent flat box or sphere puffs, colour #C79A74 at 40% alpha, shorter and redder than Kano's pale dust. In harmattan weeks tint the whole far field instead (haze). Optionally a column of smoke from burning grass at the road edge in the late dry season [inf].
- **go-slow:** a jam of keke, minibuses and a trailer at a railway crossing or roundabout: lowered yellow-and-black striped boom (thin box) with a long goods train (a row of boxes) crossing slowly, or a stopped rank of keke and painted buses with drivers' arms out. A flyover pier behind it signals a city junction.
- **cart-weave:** wheelbarrows and handcarts loaded with onions, yams, charcoal bags or jerrycans weaving across lanes at the market edge; plus a loaded tricycle truck. Use two-wheeled box carts with a cyl axle and 2-3 coloured sphere/box loads. No cattle, no donkeys driven by herders.

## 12. Design brief
**What makes Kaduna visibly different from Kano** (both are `sahel-savanna`): Kano = mud-brick compounds and earthen walls, flat roofs, dye pits, baobab/date palm, warm tan sky and ground, no trees of size. Kaduna = a planned colonial grid with big trees on both sides, hipped/pitched tile roofs behind garden walls, a rail line and a steel truss bridge, a visible river, granite hills, a cooler blue-grey sky and red-brown ground, wide dual avenues with medians, flyover piers and glowing roundabouts, and only a thin strip of mud compounds at the edge.

**Palette (hex)**
- sky `#AFC4D4` (cooler than Kano's tan); fog `#E4E8E4`; ground `#9B6A4C` (laterite red-brown, less orange than Kano); accent `#B9663A` (burnt clay; **replace the pack's saturated red `#C0392B`**, which reads as a party colour).
- Building colours: limewash cream `#EADFC6`, whitewash `#F2EEE2`, sandcrete grey `#B8B2A2`, tile terracotta `#A5573A`, dusty ochre `#CDA66B`, zinc grey `#98A2A5`, shutter slate blue `#4F6D85`.
- Foliage: neem/mango `#5E8A48`, dry-season grass `#C9B777`, hill rock `#8D8A84`, river `#7C8F8B`.
