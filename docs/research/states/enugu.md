# Enugu — world research dossier

Status: DRAFT (research only; not reviewed by a local reviewer). Every landmark stays `verified: false` until someone from the state signs off in section 14.

Rules that bind this dossier (AGENTS.md, docs/GUARDRAILS.md, docs/chunks/C27.md, C34.md, ADR-0009):
- No photos are downloaded, traced, copied or committed. Record a text description and the source URL only.
- A venue is one of: market, stadium, square, park, bridge, landmark, waterfront. A palace is scenery only, never a venue. No religious building as a landmark. Nothing named after a person. No recent disaster. No real politicians, parties, slogans or party colours.
- Welcome and farewell lines only in a language the state actually speaks.

House rule applied (as in the Kwara and Borno dossiers): no real person's name is written below. A hill, stadium, square, avenue or airport named for a person is called "person-named" and described by role; the reviewer can identify it. Source URLs whose slug contains a person's name are listed by publisher and headline gist (URL withheld). Parties, movements, governors and slogans are described generically.

Evidence tags: **[2+]** two or more independent sources agree; **[1]** single source, unconfirmed; **[inf]** my inference or general knowledge no fetched source confirmed.

## 1. Scope and date
Researched 2026-10-27 by one agent using WebSearch (standard, plus extended for hillside housing) and WebFetch on Wikipedia (Enugu city, Enugu State, Udi-Nsukka Plateau, Udi Hills, Ngwo Pine forest, Iva Valley, Ogui, New Haven, Nike, Polo Park Mall), Wikivoyage (Enugu, Igbo phrasebook), Wikimedia Commons (category and file pages: titles, captions, categories only; no image opened), Nigerian news and government releases, tourism blogs and an academic abstract. Many news pages carry misleading "2026" page-render dates; incident dates below are given only where article text or a byline states them. Several outlets (Guardian Nigeria, Africa Check, Libcom) refused fetches (403/429) and are marked "search summary only". The search tool is US-only; Nigerian coverage is patchy.

**Headline findings (read first; they change the draft pack):**
1. **Only one of the five draft landmarks survives, and only as a renamed stand-in.** Ogbete Main Market had a 26 Jul 2023 protest with reported deaths and a small fire on 2 Jun 2025 [2+]. The hill ("Milken Hill") is named for a colonial engineer. The stadium is person-named. "Coal Camp" is a spare-parts trade area with a Dec 2023 fatal fire and a government relocation order; the Iva Valley mine beside it is the site of the 1949 miners' shooting. Only Nike Lake survives, with an area-security flag (section 8).
2. **Nearly every named Enugu market has a recent incident.** Ogbete (2023 riot, 2025 fire), the auto-parts market at Coal Camp (Dec 2023 fire), the two Artisan markets (2021 armed clash over demolition; a fire), Gariki (a killing and a burnt building), Kenyatta (demolished). The market slot therefore rests on the weakest-evidence option (New Haven Market) with a role-named generic fallback (section 8).
3. **A Monday stay-at-home order by a separatist group still affects Enugu trade** (traders say Monday patronage is low in early 2026 [2]). Shuttered-shop or empty-street imagery reads as that order; avoid it.
4. **Okada is banned in the metropolis** (law 2012; enforcement drive 2022) [2+]. Keke is allowed but the state announced in Dec 2025 that it will be barred from ten named corridors under a new BRT scheme; whether this is in force is unconfirmed (section 4).
5. **The Enugu zoo is reported closed** (2024 reporting; one report says converted to an estate) [2: search summaries]; it is not a candidate.
6. **Enugu has a new landmark that no earlier state has**: a 400 m pedestrian sky bridge joining two bus terminals (commissioned Aug 2025) [2+], but it is a flagship of the sitting governor's administration (POL-adjacent).

## 3. Built form
Overall character [2+: S1, S2, S15]: **Enugu is a young city (1909-1917 coal camp) that climbs a hillside**, not an old walled town. The look is stepped terraces of block houses on slopes, a leafy colonial quarter on the ridge-top, a flat commercial centre in the valley, and a flat-topped escarpment always on the horizon. Players should feel gradient, rust zinc against grey-green hills and coal-black workshops.

### 3a. Old and vernacular
- **Village Igbo compound [2: S9, S31]:** Ogui, Nike and Ngwo are older village communities absorbed by the city. Historically mud/laterite walls and thatch round a courtyard with an obi entrance room; now cement-plastered block, corrugated zinc, square columns. First hilltop European camp used "Igbo traditional mud housing" [1: S30]. Use sparingly.
- **Coal-camp hillside settlement [2: S1, S15]:** squatter camps grew up the slopes of the Udi hills near the mines. Modelling reading [inf]: low block houses, rust-zinc or faded-green-grey zinc roofs, stepped up a slope with concrete stairways and retaining walls, laundry lines, water tanks. Abandoned-mine erosion cracks foundations here [1: search summary of a 2019 report]; **do not show cracks, subsidence, mine mouths or collapsed ground**.
- **Face-me-I-face-you [1: S1]:** low rental flats around a shared yard entrance; a good cheap street-edge lot [inf form].

### 3b. Colonial and mid-century
- **GRA and hill-top quarter [2: S1, S2, S6]:** the former European quarter north of the Ogbete River; narrow winding roads; Georgian and Victorian "English cottage" houses adapted to the tropics; old government buildings and mansions. Model [inf]: single or one-and-half storey hipped cottage, deep veranda on posts, stone or brick plinth, high-pitched hipped zinc or tile roof, chimney stub, mature shade trees behind a low wall.
- **Mid-century modern [1: S1]:** a six-storey bank tower (late 1950s) was the tallest in the business district; a 100-room hotel opened 1963. Model [inf] as a slab tower with a vertical fin or brise-soleil grid, flat roof, rooftop signage; invent the bank's name.
- **Railway [2: S1, S15]:** Eastern Line to the port completed 1916; the station sits beside the stadium on the Ogui road [1: S1]. Passenger status unconfirmed (no source found). Form is [inf].
- **Do not model:** the cathedral by the main market, the government house (known as the "Lion Building" [1: S9 wiki]), the assembly, the national museum (it holds civil-war artefacts [1: search summary]).

### 3c. Contemporary city
- **Business district and high streets [2: S1, S10]:** laid-out 1960s suburb turned commercial along a person-named avenue; malls (three floors, 22,500 m2 [1: S25]); hotels; banks; a tech scene [1: S1]. Use plaza-block, shophouse, tiled-villa.
- **New transport district [2+: S16, S17]:** two terminals in the cathedral-adjacent district joined by a 400 m pedestrian sky bridge; food courts, banking halls, shops, cinemas; CNG refuelling; 80+ new bus shelters; BRT lanes; electronic fare cards [S16]. Form [inf]: long enclosed box on slim piers over a road, glazed or louvred, with two terminal slabs.
- **Flyovers under construction [2: S32]:** Abakpa, Emene twin, Nike T-junction; Eke-Obinagu flyover nearly complete. Generic flyover piers are safe as skyline-edge scenery; a flyover/bridge on the expressway collapsed in 2023 [1-2: S32]: never show cracked or broken bridge parts.
- **Public art at roundabouts [2: S20, search summary]:** statues and sculptures stand on several roundabouts, e.g. a masquerade statue unveiled Dec 2024; **a masquerade is a ritual figure; keep roundabout art abstract** [inf].
- **Open drains [inf]:** concrete channels along kerbs; steep slopes make them fast gutters.
- Industry [1: S1]: brewing, bottling, pharma, cement, vehicle assembly on the city outskirts; model as factory-shed lots; invented brands only.

## 4. Road
- **Surface [inf]:** dark asphalt, patched on slopes, with pale sandstone-grey dust washed onto the shoulder after rain. Rural links edged by red-brown and pale sandy soil [S4, S29].
- **The hill road [2: S12]:** a 3 km stretch cut from solid rock with about 28 sharp bends (sources differ: 28 to 33), clinging to the cliff with steep drops, street lights installed in the rebuilding, pine and tropical trees forming a shaded tunnel, city panorama from the bends. It is a section of a 12 km federal road that also serves as a bypass towards the Onitsha expressway. Okada reportedly use it [1].
- **City roads [2: S1, S2]:** narrow winding roads in the GRA; dual carriageways with planted medians on the main avenues [inf]; roundabouts with sculpture [S20]. Named avenues are mostly person-named (call them "the main avenue", "the ring road").
- **Highways [2: S1]:** A3 to Port Harcourt (1970s), A232 to Onitsha, A343 to Abakaliki.
- **Poles, wires, signs [inf]:** concrete poles, bundled sagging wires, junction billboards, green direction signs, new LED street lights on the rebuilt hill road [S12].
- **BRT [S16, S17]:** lane markings and bus shelters are a new visual: shelters with a roof slab on two posts and a long bench; invented route numbers.
- **Transport on the street:**
  - **Okada: banned.** A 2012 law banned okada in the metropolis (one year in jail, no fine) [2: S18]; Wikipedia says April 2009 [1: S1]; a July 2022 law/enforcement drive covers the three city LGAs [1: search summary]. Treat okada as absent in the city. The hill-road blog says okada use it [1: S12], which conflicts; reviewer to settle (Q12).
  - **Keke:** present (about 18,000 riders statewide [S16]). A Feb 2022 order banned keke, okada and tippers in several areas and was lifted a week later [S18]. In Dec 2025 the state announced keke would be barred from ten named corridors for BRT, with keke kept for last-mile feeder work [S16]; 40,000 drivers protested; launch date unset. Keke body colour in Enugu: **not found**.
  - **Taxi:** registered taxis are yellow, and "yellow-and-black" is the official scheme but many have vanished [2: S1, S3, S19]; unregistered "kabu-kabu" lack yellow paint [2: S1, S3].
  - **Yellow minibuses (Hiace type) [2: S16, S19]**; government-supplied small buses; the new CNG buses [S17].
- **Roadside activity [1 each, S3, S20]:** welders and metal workers (Coal Camp), spare-parts stalls, food stalls (abacha, nkwobi, okpa), palm wine sellers, tyre repairers [inf], garri sellers (garri is a main New Market line [S1]).

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- **Zones [2: S2, S6]:** derived savanna and forest mosaic; the city lies at the **foot of an east-facing escarpment** (flat-topped, steep face, Ajali sandstone over shale/coal), not on a single hill [S1, S4]. Highest point of the state 592 m on the ridge, city 180-240 m [S1, S2, S6]. Beyond the city: small round-topped hills and grassland [S6].
- **Rock and soil [2: S1, S4, S29]:** sandstone on top, shale low down; poor sandy acidic soil; severe erosion and gullies on slopes (80-96% sand in an Opi road study). Colour [inf]: iron-stained red-brown and pale yellow-grey sandstone cut banks, grey-black shale and coal dust near the old camps. Granite and sandstone boulders on the Udi hills [1: S5].
- **Trees [partly sourced]:** pine (colonial reserve plantations at Ngwo [2: S11, S12]), oil palm [2: S2, S6], raffia (sacred in the cave area [1: S11]; keep as a plain palm), mango, ube, ukwa and bamboo [inf], palm wine trees [1: S3 nkwu enu], banana and pawpaw [inf]; tree-lined resort grounds [S8]; patchy trees near homes and cleared farmland [S4].
- **Crops [2: S2, S4]:** yam, cassava, maize, rice, cocoyam, palm oil; yam barns as in Anambra [inf].
- **Water [2: S1, S7]:** Ekulu, Asata, Ogbete and other streams in valley floors; **Nike Lake** the city's largest lake with boating and wild birds [S7]; small crocodile lake at another site [1: S7]; waterfalls (Ngwo cave fall, Awhum 30 m) are religious or remote and not used.
- **Climate [2: S1, S3]:** Aw; humid, rainfall about 1,900-2,000 mm; hot all year; March-November humid; harmattan dust for a few weeks in Dec-Jan.
- **Light by season:** Rains Apr-Oct: low cloud on the ridge, wet slopes. **Early dry Nov: clear mornings, haze in valleys**, which suits the layered-ridge idea. Harmattan Dec-Jan: pale dusty sky. The calendar unlocks Enugu on 9 Nov 2026, so default to **early dry season with valley mist and a faint haze**.
- **Hillsides at city edge [inf]:** coal-black mine spoil mounds, grass-grown; do not show open mine entrances (S7 says old mines are closed and unsafe; S15).

## 6. Dress
Sources are consistent on garment names; mixing for daily life is [inf].
- **Men, everyday [2: S27 + Anambra dossier]:** T-shirt, shirt and trousers/jeans; ankara shirt; buba/kaftan; suit-and-tie in the business district [inf]; sandals, sneakers, slippers.
- **Women [2: S27]:** puff-sleeve or off-shoulder blouse with one or two wrappers (George, Ankara, Akwete); ichafu/gele headtie; lace blouses; market women in wrappers, aprons, headties, basins on head [inf]. Hijab and long gowns for the Hausa-speaking minority around Gariki/Awkunanaw [S2, S24; inf for dress].
- **Working [1 each, S20, S14]:** welders in boiler suits with face-shield flipped up; mechanics in oil-stained overalls; spare-parts boys; bus conductors with a satchel; bricklayers and porters with trolleys; keke drivers.
- **Children [inf]:** school uniforms (check shirt/tunic), sandals; do not show a mission/church school badge.
- **Avoid list (hard):**
  1. Party colours as clothing, caps, wraps, umbrellas, keke bodies, canopies: **#D4621B, #1FA98F, #3B6FD4, #F5CE2E, #C23FA0, #8257C8**. Keep fabric swatches >48 RGB from each. The yellow taxi livery must be a dull ochre, never saturated yellow, never in a trio with white.
  2. **The red chief's cap (okpu ozo) and leopard cap (okpu agu)**, and any solid red cap: they read as chieftaincy and as political caps [S27]. Do not use the isiagu lion-head shirt in a crowd; if used, rare, muted charcoal/cream and never with a red cap or a staff.
  3. **A red-black-green trio** on flags, sashes, banners, kekes or signs; with a rising-sun or sun disc it reads as a separatist flag [inf]. The pack accent (charcoal) plus green foliage must never be combined into stripes with red.
  4. **Separatist and civil-war imagery:** flags, sun symbols, slogans, memorials, uniforms; Monday shuttered-shop scenes; a market gate with the word "Biafra" or "Free". The Monday stay-at-home pattern is current [S22].
  5. **Masquerade and ritual:** mmanwu/ijele figures, ofo staffs, shrine objects, cave offerings, masks on roundabout art [S3, S11, S20].
  6. **Religious dress and buildings:** clerical collars, choir robes, white-garment processions, prayer lines; no cathedral, church spire, cross, minaret or dome in any skyline, even far off (a cathedral sits beside the main market and the new terminals).
  7. **Uniformed forces and armed groups:** police, army, neighbourhood-watch and forest-guard uniforms, camouflage, checkpoints, weapons, kidnap or burning imagery (S21).
  8. **Herder-coded imagery and ethnic pairing:** cattle drives, staff-carrying herdsmen, cattle-market scenes (the 2021 Artisan clash was with cattle traders [S24]); no "Igbo vs Hausa" labelling.
  9. **Miners as victims or heroes:** no hard hats with injuries, no memorial statue, no "1949" text.
  10. **Slogans and branding:** the state's nickname used as a slogan, the state broadcaster, state government project names ("Ije card", "Coal City ..." schemes); real company names; use invented brands.

## 7. Signs and language

## 11. Hazard visual notes

- **hilly-road:** a steep climb shown by a rising road plane and a stack of layered ridge silhouettes receding upward; loaded keke slowing; a convex mirror on a pole; a few pale rock-cut shoulders. Cheap build: 3 tilted road slabs (ramp), a pale sandstone cut-bank wedge `#CDB98F` and 3-4 pine cones; no vehicle tipping.
- **switchback:** a hairpin on the rock-cut hill road [S12]: chevron boards (6 alternating off-white `#E8E6DA` and charcoal `#34383B` plates) on the outside, a low concrete-post rail (box posts + thin rail), cut-bank wall inside (exists as `cutbank` op), pine canopy overhead, city roofs far below the lip. - **go-slow:** a knot of yellow minibuses and kabu-kabu taxis and loaded keke at a terminal ramp or the foot of a flyover pier; hawkers with trays; spare-parts barrows; a hand-painted "Go-Slow" board; amber hazard blinks. ## 12. Design brief
**Distinctness from the nine existing worlds.** Lagos (lagoon metro), Kano/Borno (pale sahel), Rivers/Delta (creek delta), Anambra (flat Niger-bank market city, laterite gullies), Kaduna (planned guinea-savanna grid), Kwara (terracotta, granite twin cone), Oyo (rust-roof sea, Yoruba, inselberg ridge). **Enugu's tells**: a long **flat-topped escarpment** wall, three layers deep, always behind the city; **stepped hillside terraces** of block houses; a **rock-cut hairpin road under pines**; pale sandstone cut banks banded with rust; coal-black workshops; cool grey-green valley mist (fog is green-grey, not cream); yellow minibuses and keke; a long glass-roofed sky bridge in the city centre.
