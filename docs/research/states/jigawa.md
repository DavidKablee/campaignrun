## 3. Built form
Hausa: mud-brick compounds with flat or domed mud roofs and ornamented parapets, now block bungalows and storey buildings in cream and ochre; walled towns with gates. Dutse: rocky hills and boulders among buildings. **Thin**: no street-level source on Dutse or Hadejia.

## 4. Road
Asphalt trunk roads (A2 north, A237 east), sand and dune edges, date palms, rail line to Nguru. Keke and okada (night curfew), minibuses (**thin**, no colour sources). The draft hazards dust-plume and cart-weave fit; go-slow is generic.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
Sudan savanna with scattered neem, acacia and date palms; granite inselbergs and boulder fields near Dutse and Kazaure; sand dunes in the north; the Hadejia wetlands with typha reeds, rice paddies and irrigation canals in the dry season. Very hot dry season, harmattan haze, a short wet season.

## 6. Dress
Hausa dress: babban riga or gown and cap (hula) for men, wrapper, hijab or veil for women, indigo cloth. Fulani herders' dress is avoided (conflict imagery). Avoid: red caps, a sarki's turban and robes, green-and-white sets that read as party colours.

## 7. Signs and language
Hausa and English (Kanuri and Fula also spoken). Board words in English; Hausa words (KASUWA, SHAGO, RUWA) need a native check.
