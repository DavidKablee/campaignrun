Search is US-only; many Nigerian news pages carry misleading 2026 crawl dates, so incident dates below come from article text only.

Honest headline: **Plateau is the hardest state so far for the landmark rules, and the draft pack mostly fails.**
1. **Jos Main Market (Terminus)** had fires in April and December 2025, a hoax "explosion" panic in Nov 2024, and the 2010 and 2014 bombings are still its best-known story [2+: S21-S24]. Katako (Nov 2024 fire) and Bukuru market (street-trade clash) are no better.
2. **Jos North was hit on the night of 29 Mar 2026** (Angwan Rukuba junction, about 28 dead per AFP), then mob violence on the University of Jos axis, Bauchi Road, Farin Gada and Tina Junction, a 48-hour curfew, and the national league moved Plateau's home matches away from Jos for "security concerns" on 4 Apr 2026 [2+: S27, S28, S20]. Attacks continued in Jos South (Bum Dec 2025, Gyel and Gura Top mid-2026), Jos East (Fusa), Bokkos, Mangu (Aug 2026), Bassa and Qua'an Pan [2+: S29]. The brief's "keep rural segments generic" applies to the whole state.
3. **The stadium the draft means is person-named** (the old township stadium); the new bowl on Zaria Road is unfinished, state-project-branded and its name unresolved [2: S17-S19]. "Rayfield Lakeside" is not a verifiable public place (a hotel brand and one SEO blog) [S47].
4. **Mining pits are not a safe venue or hazard image.** More than 4,000 abandoned ponds, dozens of drownings [S31]; pit collapses and armed attacks at mining sites in 2026 (Kuru, Gyel, Kassa) [S30]; a statewide mining suspension since Feb 2025 [S30].

Consequence: I recommend the Borno approach (section 8): two real, low-flag natural/public places (**Jos Wildlife Park Gate**, **Shere Hills**) plus **three role-named generic venues** (market, roundabout square, township stadium) so no real market, junction or ground is used. Decision for the project owner is in section 13 (Q1).

## 3. Built form
**Overall:** a colonial tin-mining city founded on cassiterite (mining from 1902, S2, S31) laid out as a grid on a plateau, now a dense low-rise sea of roofs filling a bowl among bare granite domes and ridges [2+: S1, S12, S15 hill view]. Wikivoyage calls it old and colonial, with isolated rock formations and old mining areas [S3]. Plateau should feel **low, pale and rocky: single-storey cream houses under silver roofs, 2-3 storey flat-roof shop strips, and always a boulder pile in the gaps**.

### 3a. Everyday housing and streets
- **Sandcrete bungalow `[obs]`:** cream/pale-yellow rendered walls (about `#E3D3A1`), a low **hipped roof of grey corrugated zinc with a deep eave**, plain dark steel doors, rubble and stacked blocks in a bare orange-tan yard, a satellite dish; mature trees behind; a granite dome on the horizon (S15, "Houses in the eastern part of the city, Jos (02)").
- **Shop strips `[obs]`:** 2-3 storey flat-roof concrete frame blocks in pastel render: mint green, dusty pink-brown, cream with terracotta upper floors, balconies with metal rails, painted signboards, concrete poles with sagging wires, dusty tan unmetalled side streets (S15 "Jos 3", "Terminus market traffic").
- **Modern block `[obs]`:** a four-storey cream block with a **curved blue-glass corner**, decorative pediments, a white painted metal fence on a low wall, bare dusty verge (S15 "Birnin Jos"). Use as the contemporary note; one small blue glass panel only.
- **Boundary walls `[1]`:** the GRA is described as leafy with rough-hewn granite houses, wide verandas and corrugated roofs, walls that incorporate natural rock (S12). A stone fence also appears in the wildlife park (S9 file name). Use a **mortared boulder wall** as the signature kerbside prop `[inf]` for shape.
- **Village edge `[1]`:** a 1970s archive caption describes clay huts with natural stone in a village near Jos; straw huts with pointed roofs at a market (S16). **Round huts are an ethnic marker risk: use none on the street**; the park's round thatched shelters (S9) may appear only as two open rest shelters inside the park.
- **GRA / hill-station zone `[2]`:** leafy and tree-lined; mid-range hotels (S12, Wikivoyage); the state government houses are in Rayfield (S1). Model as 1-2 storey stone-and-render houses behind boulder walls with big trees. **No government-house, no named hotel** (a hill-station hotel and a plateau hotel exist; brand names, skip).
- **Palace `[2]`:** the traditional ruler's palace is in Jos North (S1). **Scenery-only and not drawn**; same for the chief's palace file in S14.
- **Industrial edge `[2]`:** Jos South holds mills, roofing sheet, breweries and steel firms (S1). Use `factory-shed`.

### 3b. Colonial and civic
- Colonial heritage is mining-era: museum compound, GRA, a hill-station hotel, a polo club and a golf course [S1]. The museum has an outdoor row of life-size replica buildings (Kano wall, a Zaria mosque, a palace, a village) [2: S1, S39]; **do not model the museum replicas** (religious and palace replicas).
- Civic blocks (banks, secretariats, hospital) appear in file names (S7). Render as 2-3 storey cream blocks with window hoods `[inf]`.
- Churches, a cathedral, a large central mosque and mission institutions all exist in the city (S1, S7). **No domes, minarets, crosses or steeples anywhere**, including the skyline.

### 3c. Contemporary city
- Wide **four-lane roads with raised kerbs and a flyover on box piers** (S15 "Flyover bridge"), double-arm street lamps (some solar `[1: S12]`), lattice telecom masts (S15 two photos), blank billboard frames (S15 Terminus Roundabout).
- Mid-rise plazas and malls are named in Wikivoyage (S3); use invented names.
- The Terminus roundabout monument `[obs]` is a cream concrete **tripod of fins carrying a round tank-like head**; use this *kind* of monument only on the generic Tower Roundabout, in a changed proportion (section 8).

### 3d. Settlement pattern
Dense grid at the core, organic sprawl towards the hills; markets and cart-pusher lanes; spacious southern suburbs (S12). Satellite trade has moved towards Jos South after clashes (S1).

## 4. Road
- **Surface `[obs + 1]`:** main roads asphalt, wide, with poured concrete kerbs and open drains and a painted edge; many side streets are **bare tan-orange dust** with potholes (S15 "Jos 3"; Wikivoyage warns of potholes, S3). Rural links: asphalt with red-orange sand shoulders `[inf]`.
- **Rocky road `[2]`:** Commons files "Beautiful Rocky road", "The Rocky road path", five "Jos Bauchi Road Mountains" photos (S14); boulders at roadside on the Jos-Bauchi route is also an archive caption (S16). Roads into the city have "steep, winding bends and mountainous scenery" [1: S1].
- **Park roads `[obs]`:** the wildlife park boulevard is a red-orange laterite track with a poured concrete edge and rain-tree avenue (S15).
- **Flyover `[obs]`:** low concrete deck on boxy piers with pedestrian space beneath, two-arm lamps down the median (S15). A real N9.9 bn Jos North flyover was flagged off in 2022 (S41): use an unnamed generic one.
- **Roundabouts `[2]`:** several; 39 crashes recorded at Jos roundabouts in a 2017 safety report (S42, old). Roundabout in the Terminus photo has a hawker belt around it (S15).
- **Poles and wires `[obs]`:** concrete poles, bundled sagging wires, lattice masts.
- **Roadside activity `[2]`:** hawkers with hand-carts of clothes and shoes, cart pushers, roadside stalls selling honey, strawberries and leather goods (S12), suya sellers at fires on cold nights (S32), kiosks and provision shops `[2: S32 names Liberty Boulevard and British America Junction trade; names withheld]`.
- **Transport `[2+]`:**
  - **Okada are banned in the Jos-Bukuru metropolis** (a long-standing master-plan rule; 72-hour order June 2025; reinforced after the March 2026 attack) [2+: S26]. **Do not use okada.** Enforcement is patchy [1], so a visible okada is still a risk.
  - **Keke (tricycles)** are common (S3, S12, S14 "Commercial tricycle in a line", S15) `[3]`; police have banned tricycles on particular days (Easter) `[1: S26]`. **In the photos the keke is a green body with a yellow roof `[obs]`.**
  - **Minibuses `[2]`:** pale grey and red Hiace-style vans (S15), and a green-and-yellow-banded one beside a house (S15 [obs]); shared taxis "yellow and green" `[1: S12]`. Vehicles are often in poor condition `[1: S3]`.
  - Checkpoints are reported on roads (S3); **never draw one**.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- **Altitude `[2+]`:** Jos 1,217 m (S1, S7 infobox); state 1,200-1,829 m (S2); plateau average about 1,280 m (S4). Shere Hills peak 1,759 m (S5), 1,776 m (S4) or 1,829 m (S5 mirror, S6) `[conflict]`: show "about 1,800 m" nowhere on screen.
- **Rock `[2+]`:** younger granite intrusions (about 160 million years) and basalt (S2, S4). **Photos `[obs]`:** (a) bare dark-grey granite slabs lying like whale backs beside paths; (b) rounded domes and piled boulders, staining from near-black to ochre; (c) hill faces of grey-brown boulder scree with sparse pale grass and a few green trees in the dry season; (d) tan-orange bare ground at the foot (S15). A famous balanced-boulder pile stands at Riyom (S1) but sits in Riyom LGA: not used.
- **Grass and trees `[2]`:** mostly open grassland now, woodland only on steep ground and riverbanks (S4, S34). **Planted trees:** pine (Pinus caribaea) in the wildlife park [2+: S8, S9 file name, S12]; eucalyptus on reclaimed tin spoil [2: S34]; Senna siamea, mango, jacaranda, plumeria, bougainvillea, lantana and tree marigold in the park (S9 file names, [1] each). Native stands include locust-bean, mahogany-type and ironwood-type trees at a nearby reserve `[1: bird site summary]`. **Do not draw jacaranda or bougainvillea in flower** (purple/magenta, party colours) or tree marigold bloom (orange).
- **Crops `[2+]`:** Irish potato (Plateau about half of national output, S33), maize, cowpea, vegetables with dry-season irrigation (S33, Britannica staples). Roadside strawberries and honey `[1: S12]`. **Terraces: not verified in any source**; draw contour ridges and mounds of potato rows `[inf]`.
- **Soil `[inf]`:** orange-tan sandy earth `[obs]` for bare ground; reddish laterite on the park track `[obs]`.
- **Water `[2]`:** abandoned tin-mine ponds (over 4,000, S31) and reservoirs (Lamingo, S38); the Plateau is the source area of the Kaduna, Gongola and Hadejia rivers (S4); small waterfalls (Mazah Hills photo `[1]`; Assop and Kurra, rejected). Pond water colour is not described in any source: use a **pale milky green `#8DB5A6`** `[inf]`.
- **Spoil `[2]`:** mining left dumps and gullies, about half of some mining settlements under dumps (S31). Use sparingly as pale-grey sandy mounds with eucalyptus on top (S34) and **no machinery, miners, night work or collapse**.
- **Climate `[2+]`:** cool Aw; mean months 21-25 C; nights to about 7 C in Dec-Jan, record-style lows near 6.7 C (Jan 2020); mid-Nov to Feb dry; harmattan dusty and cold (S1, S12, S32); rain peak July-August with hail sometimes (S1, S12); about 2,669 sunshine hours (S1). Photos `[obs]` show tall cumulus over the city in the rains and a pale milky-grey sky in the dry haze.
- **Mist `[brief claim, verify]`:** no Jos-specific source reports street-level fog. A road-safety body cites rainy-season fog nationally `[1]`. Treat mist as **early-morning ground haze and harmattan haze** `[inf]` rather than a daily fog bank.
- **Default season for the Nov 9 unlock:** early harmattan: cool clear mornings (about 12-15 C), a thin dust haze, grass still green-gold, bare crop stubble and potato rows.

## 6. Dress
Sources on Jos dress are thin; the cold is documented, the garments are `[inf]` from photos and general northern/central dress.
- **Cold-weather layers `[2]`:** residents wear layers, jackets and stockings on harmattan nights and mornings (S32, Wikivoyage on cold). Knitted caps are `[inf]`, not sourced: use **plain knit beanies in dull stone, charcoal, oatmeal or brown** (never red, green, white, yellow, orange, purple, blue as a solid), no logos, and never identical across a crowd.
- **Men `[obs]`:** shirts and trousers, fleece or puffer jackets in muted colours, long gowns (kaftan style) and caps in towns; labourers in overalls; market men in jerseys. **Women `[obs]`:** wrappers and tops, headties, long robes with head veils in town (the photos include mustard/orange and purple robes: **repaint away from party hues**), cardigans over wrappers `[inf]`. **Children `[inf]`:** school uniform check dresses, jerseys, rubber boots in rain.
- **Working dress `[1 each]`:** cart pushers in jerseys and flip-flops or boots; vegetable sellers in wrappers with plastic aprons `[inf]`; suya and charcoal sellers in jackets near the fire (S32, S16 charcoal market).
- **Footwear:** boots, sandals, trainers, stockings with sandals `[1: S32]`.
- **Palette for dress (hex):** stone `#B6AE9C`, charcoal `#4A4E52`, oatmeal `#D8CDB4`, clay `#9A6B4C`, forest dull `#56665A`, faded indigo `#4E5E7A`, plum-brown `#6B4A52`, mustard-dull `#B89A52`. - **Avoid list (hard):**
  1. Party colours as clothing, caps, wraps, umbrellas, canopies, kekes, buses: the six above and saturated red, green, white, blue. The green-and-yellow keke and minibus is real `[obs]` but must be muted: body `#5F7F55`, roof/panel `#C9A94A`; never green+yellow+white.

  5. Uniformed forces: no police, army, civil-defence, vigilante or task-force uniforms, camouflage, checkpoints, tapes, sandbags, sirens or helmets.

  7. Violence aftermath: burnt buildings, burnt vehicles, tyres on fire, rubble piles, smoke columns, bandaged people, mourning dress.
  8. Real brands and bank logos (the photos show real banks and mills): invented only.
