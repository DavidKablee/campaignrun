# Ogun — world research dossier

Status: DRAFT (research only; not reviewed by a local reviewer). Every landmark stays `verified: false` until someone from the state signs off in section 14.

Rules that bind this dossier (AGENTS.md, docs/GUARDRAILS.md, docs/chunks/C27.md, C34.md, ADR-0009):
- No photos are downloaded, traced, copied or committed. Record a text description and the source URL only.
- A venue is one of: market, stadium, square, park, bridge, landmark, waterfront. A palace is scenery only, never a venue. No religious building as a landmark. Nothing named after a person. No recent disaster. No real politicians, parties, slogans or party colours.
- Welcome and farewell lines only in a language the state actually speaks.

House rule applied (same as the Oyo dossier): no real person's name and no politician is written anywhere below. Places named after a person are called "person-named" and described by role. Where a source URL or title contains a person's name it is listed by publisher only (URL withheld). Traditional-ruler titles are not written; palaces are called "a palace".

Evidence tags: **[2+]** two or more independent sources agree; **[1]** single source, treat as unconfirmed; **[inf]** inference or general knowledge that no fetched source confirmed.

## 1. Scope and date
Researched 2026-10-27 by one agent. Routes: WebSearch (standard; extended four times) and WebFetch on Wikipedia, Wikivoyage, Wikimedia Commons category pages (file names and captions only; no image opened), Nigerian news and trade press, a building-trade/real-estate guide, an asylum-agency security report (search summary only; the page itself returned 404), travel blogs and a culture-trip piece. Pages that refused or failed (403/404/401/429/timeout): Channels TV 2013 flyover page, Radisson Olumo page, Lonely Planet Olumo page, a travel blog (429), aaroads wiki, Hive blog, Culture Trip, a Wikipedia mirror. For these only search-result summaries were seen and claims are tagged accordingly.

Honest headline:
1. **Ogun is not one look but three, and each is well sourced as text.** (a) Abeokuta: grey granite masses breaking through red laterite, houses wedged among boulders, a rust-and-zinc roof sea, the Ogun River through the lower city `[2+: S1, S9, S10]`. (b) The Lagos-Ibadan / Lagos-Abeokuta industrial corridor: cement, breweries, consumer-goods plants, quarries, a pale reinforced-concrete expressway being laid `[2+: S2, S18, S19, S21]`. (c) The forest-belt towns (Ilaro, Ijebu): timber, cocoa, kola, rubber, garri `[2: S2, S17]`.
2. **The rust-roof sea is shared with Oyo** (Abeokuta's travel copy says the same thing `[2: S9]`). To keep Ogun visibly different: more **silver zinc and grey** than rust, **grey granite boulders in every skyline**, **pale concrete road** on the corridor, **indigo cloth** in the market, **white/grey factory sheds and silos**, and no hill-and-valley road tilt (Abeokuta is 66 m; its rocks are domes, not ridges `[1: S1]`).
3. **The five draft landmarks mostly fail**: Olumo Rock is a living shrine site (decision below: skyline only); Kuto Market had a July 2024 fire and its formal name is person-or-ruler-named; the Abeokuta stadium is person-named and "Abeokuta Sports Ground" is not a name any source uses; "Itoku Adire Row" is an invented name (the real thing is the Kemta/Itoku adire market and the Adire Mall); "Ogun River Crossing" is a generic label, not a named bridge.
4. **No place in Ogun is incident-free in the wider region**: cult violence (Abeokuta North/South, Sagamu, Ikenne, Odogbolu) `[1: S25]`, kidnapping on the Lagos-Ibadan, Sagamu-Ijebu Ode and Abeokuta-Ibadan roads and a polytechnic abduction in Remo North (Aug 2026) `[2: S25]`, tanker fires on the Sagamu-Abeokuta axis (Oct 2025) and under the Sagamu interchange bridge (Dec 2019) `[2: S22]`. Rural and corridor segments are therefore generic scenery with no school, uniform, checkpoint or crash imagery.

## 3. Built form

### 3a. Abeokuta: granite and laterite (signature)
- **Terrain** `[2+: S1, S9, S10]`: wooded savanna broken by masses of grey granite; rounded domes, crevices and small caves; the red laterite earth around them; city sits on both banks of a river at only 66 m. The famous dome is 137 m asl, about 90 m above the city. **Modelling reading** `[inf]`: low whaleback domes and boulder piles 10-40 m high, grey `#A29B92` with darker streaks, scattered between streets, not a continuous ridge like Ibadan. Add a single dark broadleaf tree growing out of a crack.
- **Houses and rock** `[1: S9; 1: S6 file "a house with a rock built into it"]`: boulders sit beside, above and inside occupied homes. Use a boulder-wall lot variant: a house whose back wall is a granite face and whose roof is partly covered by an overhanging block.
- **Roofs** `[2: S9, S4]`: "sea of rusted tin roofs" from the top; another tour page says "red roofs". Treat as rust-and-zinc with a **larger silver-zinc share than Oyo** `[inf]`.
- **Old quarter** `[2: S1, S10]`: restored colonial-era buildings and Egba compounds around the rock and a palace; wall remnants (the wall was 18 miles / 29-30 km); old houses with stucco, arched doorways and balconies in the Afro-Brazilian style are reported for Abeokuta `[1: study snippet]` and for Sagamu (a colonial Brazilian-style old building `[2: S18]`). Keep Afro-Brazilian to a small share `[inf]`.
- **Rules for the rock itself**: no stairs, steps, elevator, cowry statues, shrine, huts, tomb, trees-on-top rituals, guides or crowd. See section 9.
- **Planned estates (GRA)** `[1: S10]`: Ibara and Kuto planned estates for professionals; hilltop GRA exists `[1: S6 file names]`. `[inf]` bungalows and duplexes behind walls, painted cream or pale grey, hipped roofs of zinc or long-span sheet.

### 3b. Adire quarter (Itoku / Kemta)
- `[2+: S14, S1, S15]` A sprawling market a few metres from the rock; thousands of women in production and sales; a 215-shop terraced **Adire Mall** (government-built after 2012 demolitions; handed to traders 2019 amid a dispute); a separate Kemta production pavilion where women sit in clusters over bowls of dye-soaked cloth; shop interiors with "walls of patterns and colours"; dye pits mentioned in a travel guide `[1: S15]`.
- **Modelling reading** `[inf]`: a two-floor terraced block of shop units with an open arcade; outside, bamboo or timber poles with long indigo cloths hung to dry in rows; low clay-lined pits or drums with indigo-black liquid (draw as dark round tubs, no people in them); cloth bundles of 5 yards stacked on counters; sewing machines at shop fronts. Cloth patterns: white circles on indigo (oniko), stitched spirals (alabere), painted/stencilled paste motifs (eleko).
- Do not show the 2016 building collapse near the market, the 2012 demolition, or the 2019 protest `[2: S14, search]`.

### 3c. Corridor: factories, cement, commuter towns
- **Sagamu** `[2: S18, S2]`: limestone makes cement the main industry; a golf course sits inside a cement company's grounds; kola collecting centre with basket and rope making (**kola baskets** are a distinctive small prop); thirteen towns joined; old Brazilian-style building; a stadium on Ikenne Road.
- **Ewekoro and Ibese** `[2: S2, S20]`: quarries feed plants over a long conveyor; one clinker kiln; a second plant at Itori. No source describes the look. `[inf]` white/grey silos and a tall chimney, a preheater tower of grey boxes, dust-grey ground, quarry benches stepping down, tipper trucks.
- **Ota and Agbara** `[2+: S19, S2]`: third-largest industrial concentration (1999), breweries, auto assembly, pharma, glass, food; industrial areas along the Lagos-Abeokuta and Idiroko roads; a road junction just north of a toll gate; very large merged markets; universities. `[inf]` long-span sheds of white and grey sheet, gatehouses, tanker-and-tipper yards, staff shuttle buses; 1-2 storey shophouses and estates between.
- **Commuter towns** (Mowe, Ibafo, Arepo, Ifo): housing estates (Commons: a named housing estate at Arepo) `[1]`; markets at Mowe, Ibafo, Siun `[1: S30]`. `[inf]` unfinished block houses, plaster walls, concrete-frame buildings with rebar sticking up, water tanks on stilts. The expressway has very large church campuses `[1: S2]`: **do not draw domes, auditoria or crosses.**
- **Block industry and quarry trade** `[1+inf: S6 "A Block Factory", S1 "Aro granite quarries", S30]`: yards of sandcrete blocks drying in rows, heaps of granite chips and sand, tipper trucks in ochre/grey.

### 3d. Forest-belt towns (Ilaro, Ijebu, Ayetoro)
- Ilaro `[1: S17]`: a town on a small hill; dense forest around it; sawmills at the edge; rivers; main trades garri/fufu, aso-oke weaving, a paint works, cement a few km away; a stadium on Idogo Road. Ijebu-Ode `[1: S12]`: three parts (Iwade, Ijasi, Porogun), garri, tobacco, cassava; a flyover in the Commons gallery. `[inf]` single-storey plastered houses under zinc, timber stacks and plank lock-ups, cocoa/kola drying mats.
- Palaces in every town (Abeokuta, Sagamu, Ijebu-Ode, Ilaro, Ota, Agbara): **scenery only and not depicted**.

### 3e. Contemporary city (Ibara - Oke-Mosan)
- `[2: S23, S1, S4]` Four-lane flyover at Ibara roundabout (2013) linking two roads; a long pedestrian bridge at Panseke; the governor's office, a presidential library with a resort, a conference hotel and malls at Oke-Mosan/Ibara; a technology hub; a hilltop estate. `[inf]` glass-fronted mid-rise blocks (3-6 storeys), hotel slabs with white fins, a mall with a flat canopy, street lights, medians.
- Do not draw the governor's office, library, resort, tech-hub signs or the stadium complex by name.

## 4. Road
- **Lagos-Ibadan expressway, Ogun section** `[2: S21]`: reinforced-concrete (rigid) pavement from Sagamu Interchange, 12 m carriageway, with pedestrian bridges, ramps and underpasses; original design was asphalt with 7.3 m lanes. So a **pale grey jointed slab road** (`#B8B6AE`) with transverse joints every ~5 m is accurate for the new sections and is the best single road-cue that separates Ogun from Oyo/Lagos. Mix with older dark asphalt patches `[inf]`.
- **Sagamu-Abeokuta road** `[1: S22]`: 42 km, reconstructed, street lights both sides, visibility "500-600 m", night markets at Siun and Kobape. `[inf]` asphalt `#55585B`, white edge lines, low concrete kerbs at junctions.
- **Lagos-Abeokuta road** `[2: S19, allAfrica Nov 2025 reconstruction news]`: busy, partly under reconstruction; heavy industrial traffic. Commons has "Heavy Vehicle on Osiele Road" `[1]`.
- **Abeokuta streets** `[inf + 1: S6 names]`: Commons lists roads and bridges but no description; roundabouts (Ijaiye, Ibara), a flyover, and several river bridges. Side streets: dark asphalt or red laterite, open concrete drains, stepped lanes where boulders block the way, lanes running round outcrops.
- **Shoulders**: red-brown laterite washing on to the edge; heaps of granite chips and sand at block yards; sandbag patches after rain `[inf]`.
- **Poles/wires/signs** `[inf]`: concrete poles with sagging wires, steel street-light poles on the Sagamu road, billboard frames at interchanges (invented brands only), green directional signs, yellow-black kerb paint only if a reviewer confirms (Lagos uses it; do not copy).
- **Roadside activity** `[inf + 2: S18, S26]`: motorcycle taxis (Abeokuta "already filled with bike riders", 2020 commissioner), tricycles, shared taxis (four passengers), minibuses (some drivers "erratic" S18); POS kiosks, pure-water sellers, roasted-corn and plantain vendors, vulcanisers, block-makers, kola and garri sellers at junctions.
- **Do not dramatise**: tanker fires (Sagamu interchange 2019; Abeokuta-Sagamu road Oct 2025), the May 2026 fatal crash at a bridge on the Lagos-Abeokuta road `[1: allAfrica]`, kidnappings on the Lagos-Ibadan, Sagamu-Ijebu Ode and Abeokuta-Ibadan roads `[2: S25]`. Use all roads as generic carriageways.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- **Climate** `[2: S1, S12]`: tropical wet-and-dry (Aw); Abeokuta ~1,246 mm, wettest Sept, driest Dec-Jan, humidity ~86%, hottest Feb (~36 C max); Ijebu-Ode ~1,715 mm. Harmattan Dec-Feb brings dust `[inf]`. Rainy season: grey-blue sky, steam off tarmac; harmattan: pale beige haze (use cement-dust haze for the corridor too).
- **Zone** `[2: S2, S1]`: lowland forest in most of the state, forest-savanna mosaic in the north, mangrove strip on the coast; Abeokuta itself is "wooded savanna" with granite `[1: S1]`. Result: **more big trees than Kaduna or Kano, fewer than Rivers**, with bare rock instead of hills.
- **Trees and crops** `[2+: S2, S17, S29 + Commons]`: cassava, cocoa, kola, oil palm, banana/plantain, maize, yam, **Ofada rice** (swamp rice; land around Owode, `[2: S2, S30]`), **rubber** (Commons: latex from a rubber tree `[1]`), oranges and pineapples (Ilaro `[1]`), iroko growing from rock (Olumo `[1: S5, S7]`), teak/gmelina plantations `[inf]`, raffia and bamboo in valleys `[inf]`, neem/mango/flamboyant street trees `[inf]`.
- **Soil**: red laterite plus grey granite grit `[2]`; limestone pale ground at quarries `[inf]`.
- **Water** `[2: S29, S1]`: the Ogun River through Abeokuta (people bathe and wash in it; waste from abattoirs), the Oyan reservoir 20 km north, streams in Ilaro, a lagoon edge in the south. **Floods**: July 2020 flood hit Kuto, Lafenwa and several other quarters `[2: S16]`; October 2024 dam release flooded estates at the Lagos border `[2: S24]`; a statewide flood alert is out for 2026 `[1: Guardian]`. **Draw the river low and brown-green among boulders; no flood scenes.**
- **Light**: rainy-season cumulus with humid haze; harmattan low sun makes granite warm grey and cement dust turn the far distance beige. A **cement-dust haze** gives the corridor a flatter, whiter look than Oyo's gold `[inf]`.

## 6. Dress
Sources are strong on garment names and adire, weak on everyday colour and children.
- **Men, everyday** `[inf]`: tees, polos, short-sleeve shirts, jeans/trousers, sandals; tipper drivers and block-yard workers in dusty work shirts, hard hats rarely; okada riders in jackets and plain helmets `[inf]`; market men in shirts and vests.
- **Men, dressy** `[2: S11, S32]`: buba + sokoto (kembe loose, sooro slim), dansiki, agbada for ceremonies, fila (abeti aja, gobi, akete). Egba men "typically" wear this set `[S11]`.
- **Women, everyday** `[2: S11, S14]`: iro (wrapper) + buba, gele, ipele, often in **adire** or ankara; market women with basins on the head; dye-workers in aprons with stained hands `[1: S14]`.
- **Adire** `[2+: S13, S14]`: indigo-dyed resist cloth; blue-black ground, white circles/spirals/paste motifs; newer pieces add vivid colours (**do not use these**); worn as aso-ebi, jackets, wrappers.
- **Ijebu festive colours** `[1: S12]`: tan sanyan and blues from pale to blue-black; age groups in matching outfits. This is a group-uniform pattern: **never use as a crowd**.
- **Children** `[inf]`: plain play clothes; no school-uniform crowds.
- **Footwear**: sandals, slippers, canvas shoes, wellington boots at farms and quarries `[inf]`.
- **Avoid list (hard)**:
  1. **Party colours**: `#D4621B #1FA98F #3B6FD4 #F5CE2E #C23FA0 #8257C8` and any near hue (red, green, blue, orange, yellow, purple) on clothes, caps, canopies, kekes, okada, flags, billboards. ## 12. Design brief

### What makes Ogun visibly different from the nine built states
Lagos = lagoon metro and yellow-black kerbs. Oyo = rust hill-city. Kano = mud-brick. Kaduna/Kwara = colonial/ pot-wall savanna. Rivers/Delta = creek, flares, stilts. Anambra = painted plazas. Borno = clay sahel. **Ogun = a pale-concrete corridor of white factory sheds, cement silos and billboards; an old river town where grey granite domes sit between zinc-and-rust roofs; an indigo adire market; red laterite and granite-chip shoulders; a low brown river among boulders; and timber/rubber/cocoa forest to the west.** Flat to gently rolling, no hill-and-valley tilt.
