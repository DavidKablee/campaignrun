## 3. Built form
Overall character `[2+]`: Anambra is the most urbanised state in the SE (about 62% urban, S4), with an almost continuous built-up belt Onitsha-Obosi-Ogidi-Nnewi-Awka (S4, S5). Growth in Onitsha was "haphazard", no zoning, slums, congestion (S5). Players should feel density, not planned avenues.

### 3a. Onitsha market-city (the signature)
- **What is sourced:** the Main Market sits on the east bank, bounded by the river on the west (S1). It was rebuilt after 1968 (S1). Stalls are "tightly constructed to accommodate as many shops as possible" and movement can be near impossible at peak (S25 summary). Traders built shops, plazas and parking on drainages outside the original master plan (S41). Specialist plazas exist inside (a phones/electronics plaza of 272 shops, S25 summary). Spec-market sections are named by trade (S1). Government proposes multi-storey rebuilds (S25), which implies the present stock is mostly lower.
- **Height:** multi-storey building is common but poorly regulated; a five-storey building and a two-storey building at Ochanja collapsed (S41). So 2-5 storeys is plausible for market plazas `[1 + inferred]`. **Do not model collapse or fire.**
- **Modelling reading `[inferred]`:** repeated blocks 2-4 storeys, flat concrete roofs with parapets and water tanks on top, plus lower sheds roofed in corrugated zinc; full-width shop fronts with roll-up shutters painted in the shop's colour; hand-lettered or vinyl fascia boards stacked above each other; narrow lanes with canopies/parasols and trolleys. Sub-market identity by goods: ceramics, textiles, stockfish, timber, glass (S1), which gives colour-by-block (stacked basins and plastic ware, bolts of cloth, stacked planks).
- **Riverside edge (historical `[1]`, S29):** high banks, concrete stairways from the canoe landing up to the market, flood marks of tens of feet, rows of market-stall buildings along the water, big dock cranes at the old trading-company wharf.
- **Bridgehead:** the old Niger Bridge approach is a congested bridgehead with informal traders (S2); Commons names a "Welcome to Onitsha head bridge" board (S18, name only).

### 3b. Contemporary city streets (Awka, Nnewi, new Onitsha edge)
- **Awka** `[2]`: capital in a valley with two ridges (S6); main commercial artery with bank branches (S6); side streets mix boutiques, upholstery, salons, mini-supermarkets, pharmacies (S13). Government-heavy skyline of secretariat blocks (S6, S19 file names). Newer flyovers and widened expressway (S6, S39). Hotels and glass-fronted commercial blocks `[inferred]`.
- **Nnewi** `[2]`: second city, industrial: motor/motorcycle parts, cables, cosmetics, palm oil, textiles (S7); a travel guide summary says no old monuments, "giant markets and modern industrial sites" (search summary of a listing site `[1]`). A major roundabout at the centre (S7, S21). Modelling reading: flat-roofed warehouse-shops, tyre and spare-parts stacks, factory sheds with saw-tooth or low-pitch roofs, high painted boundary walls, company signboards. A claim that Nkwo Nnewi shops were built without wood and with concrete deck roofs came from a search summary I could not re-confirm: `[1, unverified]`.
- Both cities: shopfronts in flat-roof "plaza" form with exposed concrete frames, tiled or painted fronts, stacked fascia signs, rooftop water tanks (black or blue plastic cylinders) `[inferred]`.

### 3c. Village compounds and big private homes
- **Traditional:** mud/laterite walls, thatch, courtyards, barns, shrines, gateways; carved gates for titled people, guardian figures flanking some entrances (S9). `[2: S9, S10]`.
- **Modern shift `[1: S31]`:** concrete blocks and cement plaster replaced mud; corrugated sheet roofs; steel or large square columns; obi reception houses sitting at the compound entrance as a gate-house with seats; uli-painted entrance walls; heavy red pigment on one obi.
- **Return-home mansions `[1 kind + inferred form]`:** hometown standard is a mansion, not a bungalow (S45); big houses in Nnewi, Nnodo, Nnobi (S45). No source gives their form. Reading: 2-4 storeys, columned porticos, balconies with balustrades, flat roofs with parapets and decorative pediments or low hipped roofs in coloured tile/sheet, tall rendered boundary walls with a big steel gate between gate pillars, sometimes statues or lions on pillars (statue/guardian tradition at S9 supports the motif, not the exact present-day form). Mark every one of these as `[inferred]`.
- **Colonial remnants `[1 + inferred]`:** Waterside held company warehouses from the 1850s and a "European Quarter" on higher ground with old colonial residences (S29, historical). Brazilian-style stuccoed storey houses with arched windows and verandas are documented for Lagos (S10), not shown for Onitsha. Colonial feel to use sparingly: single-storey bungalow with deep veranda, hipped zinc roof, louvred shutters, near the GRA.
- **Rural settlement:** homesteads along cleared paths to a central meeting place (S10); scattered compounds in farmland `[2: S10, S9]`.

### 3d. Gully landscape (scenery, not a venue)
Nanka/Agulu/Oko: a deep gorge cutting through villages; homes about 30 m from the edge; structures have fallen in (S32). Reported size about 66 m deep, 2.9 km long, 349 m wide `[1: S33]`, "roughly 100 m deep" in another summary `[1]`; the numbers disagree, so only "very deep, kilometre-scale" is safe. Soil is about 98% sand `[1]`, so walls are pale-to-red sand banks `[colour: S33 says red, 1 source]`. Cashew and pine were planted on the slopes (S32, S8 `[2]`). Hazard note: landslides are ongoing and have killed people (S8), so do not use as a venue or show collapse.

## 4. Road
Little is documented; flagged accordingly.
- **Surface `[inferred]`:** asphalt, dark grey, patched; on rural links often worn at edges with sand/laterite washing onto the carriageway after rain. Source hints: file name "bad roads after rain" (S19), a government road reported washed away by rainfall (search headline), complaints about Owerri Road and Upper Iweka (S41 summary).
- **Express roads `[1]`:** the A232 through Awka was widened with three flyovers between Amawbia and Amansea (S6); another report says double lanes on the rehabilitated Amawbia to Agu Awka stretch with street lights (S39). Treat lane count (2 vs 3) as unresolved. Flyovers at Amawbia, Kwata and Aroma junctions (S39 `[2: S6, S39]`).
- **Second Niger Bridge approach `[1 summary]`:** dual carriageway, three lanes each way, about 37 m wide, concrete median barrier, toll plaza with canopies (S24). A new approach to the bridge has a toll station near Obosi/Onitsha (S3).
- **Old Niger Bridge `[2]`:** carriageway 11 m in the centre between the trusses, pedestrian walkway each side (S2, S23); congested with vehicles and informal traders (S2).
- **Roundabouts `[2]`:** Nnewi city centre (S7, S21), Amawbia and Arroma junction (S19 names), Onitsha person-named roundabout (S18 name), Ekwulobia roundabout (festive bottleneck, S40), a roundabout on the route into Nkisi Road (S35).
- **Drains `[1]`:** open gutters beside Onitsha streets, many shown blocked/dirty (S18 file names). Model as an open concrete channel strip at the kerb edge.
- **Kerbs/median:** painted kerb stones and planted medians on the new city roads `[inferred]`; the Awka rehabilitated stretch is street-lit (S39).
- **Roadside activity:** tricycles (keke) and okada on town edges, minibuses between towns (S34); Nnewi: motorcycles primary, shuttles and tricycles (S7); large inter-city mass-transit bus parks (Chisco, JC and others, S18 names); commercial buses loading on the expressway at Upper Iweka causing hold-ups (S41 summary). Trucks and tankers are common `[inferred]`. A keke restriction/curfew existed in some LGAs (S39), so keke presence is normal but time-limited.
- **Poles, wires, signs `[inferred, no source]`:** concrete or wooden power poles with sagging low-voltage wires, billboards at junctions, directional signs in green/white or blue; a "Welcome to Onitsha" head-bridge board is named at S18.
- **Red-earth shoulders `[inferred + 1]`:** laterite shoulders and erosion cuts along rural links; Anambra is a leading erosion state (S4, S5).

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- **Climate `[2]`:** tropical wet-and-dry; rainy season roughly Mar/Apr to Oct, dry season Nov to Mar with Harmattan haze (S5, S6). Temps 26-31 C (S5, S17). Humid sky, white-grey cumulus, low sun haze in dry months.
- **Crops `[2]`:** oil palm, maize, rice, yam, cassava (S4, state history page in search summary); washed bitter-leaf export (S4). Rice on the Anambra River irrigation farm at Omor (search summary of Wikipedia Omor `[1]`). Yam barns (oba ji): vertical posts, horizontal poles, tubers tied in rows with raffia, open to the air, barn size = status `[2: S14, S44]`.
- **Trees `[partly sourced]`:** oil palm and raffia (state seedling programme for raffia and oil palm, search summary `[1]`); cashew and pine on the gully slopes (S32, S8); mango, breadfruit, pear-like ube, banana, plantain are standard Igbo homestead trees `[inferred, general knowledge]`. A rainforest remnant exists at Awka (S6) and Ogbunike's valley is dense rainforest (S11).
- **Terrain `[2]`:** low hills and cuestas in Awka (S6), valley and hill at Ogbunike (S11), Onitsha at the western end of the central Anambra hills (S5); sandy, leached, erosion-prone soils (S7).
- **Water `[2]`:** the River Niger at Onitsha on the west; Anambra (Omambala) river floodplain north-west; Agulu Lake (S4). Niger water is brown-green with sandbanks and sand-dredging canoes and small boats on the banks at Onitsha South (search summary of sand-mining reports `[1]`). Water level is seasonal (S27).
- **Soil colour `[1 + inferred]`:** red laterite for rural roads and cuts; gully walls red and pale sand (S33). Dry-season dust coats leaves.

## 6. Dress
Sources are weak and partly commercial; the garment names are consistent across sources, the colour/pattern details are not.
- **Men, everyday `[inferred]`:** short-sleeve shirts, trousers, polo shirts, sandals; traders in vests/T-shirts; traders and drivers in work-worn clothes. **Festive/ceremonial `[2: S15, S30]`:** isiagu (velvet pullover with lion-head pattern) with trousers or wrapper, traditionally restricted to titled men and still ceremonial and expensive. Caftan/agbada-type outfits in plain pastel `[inferred]`.
- **Women `[2: web summaries of Igbo dress]`:** blouse (puff-sleeved or off-shoulder) with one or two wrappers (George, Ankara, Akwete, Abada), headtie or scarf, coral-bead necklaces especially for weddings and festivals. Market women: wrapper and blouse in prints, headscarf, apron, plastic basins on the head `[inferred]`.
- **Children `[inferred]`:** school uniforms (checked or plain blue/white/green frocks and shorts, a sign of the SE school culture; avoid exact colour pairs, see avoid list), sandals, play clothes.
- **Working people `[inferred]`:** keke and bus drivers in short-sleeve shirts; porters with trolleys and head pads; mechanics and spare-parts boys in overalls; yam farmers in faded work shirts, wellington boots or sandals.
- **Footwear/headwear:** sandals, flip-flops; sun hats and caps for farmers (plain, no insignia); headscarves.
- **Avoid list (hard):**
  1. The red cap (Igbo title cap, okpu agu / okpu ododo): reads as a political cap and as a chieftaincy marker. Do not use red caps at all, even on elders.
  2. Isiagu in the black-and-gold plus red-cap combination, or any look that reads as one particular figure's trademark outfit. If isiagu is shown, use a single non-political colour (indigo, wine, ochre), no red cap, no ofo staff.
  3. Any shirt, cap, wrapper, flag, umbrella, broom, horse, eagle or tree emblem that echoes a party livery; avoid red-white, green-white, red-green-white, blue-red-white as a trio on clothing, hats, kekes, stall canopies or flags. Review against current party liveries with the local reviewer.
  4. Biafra or separatist symbols: red-black-green tricolour stripes, a half-sun emblem, "Biafra" text, sit-at-home themes (shutters closed on a Monday). None.
  5. Religious dress or buildings: clerical collars, church choir robes, mosque forms, crosses, shrines, masquerade costumes (Ijele and others) and ritual objects (staffs, gongs, skulls).
  6. Palaces and traditional rulers' regalia (scenery only, and not even that for this state).
  7. Weapons, uniformed security with guns, protest or police-bribe scenes (a Head Bridge incident in Aug 2026 is in the news; keep it out).

**Best five and why (fit all rules, differ visually from each other, one per place):**
1. **Onitsha Main Market** (segment 0): the state's identity; dense block silhouette with a roof slab and tower.
2. **Second Niger Bridge** (segment 1): reads as modern concrete, so Delta's old steel truss and Anambra's new box girder feel different; both bridges can share the bank view. If the lead wants the old bridge, take "Niger Bridge (Onitsha head)" and flag the Delta duplication.
3. **Onitsha River Port** (segment 2): gives the pack's `river barges` scenery something real to land on; crane-and-barge silhouette is unlike any market or road.
4. **Nnewi City Centre Roundabout** (segment 3): the contemporary city district with keke and spare-parts commerce; replaces the unverifiable Ekwulobia Square.
5. **Awka Township Stadium** (segment 4): clean stadium silhouette, state capital; matches C34's generic "<City> Township Stadium" naming.
Pack changes suggested: "Bridgehead Crossing" -> Second Niger Bridge; "Ekwulobia Square" -> Nnewi City Centre Roundabout; "Nkisi Park" -> Onitsha River Port (waterfront; Tracas Park stays as a park alternate); "Awka Civic Ground" -> Awka Township Stadium. ## 11. Hazard visual notes
- **market-spill:** loaded handcart tips and spills a fan of coloured basins, folded cloth bolts and stacked buckets (market sections are by trade: ceramics, textiles, timber, S1); sacks of rice/garri; a tyre rolling from Nnewi stacks. Cheap: 6-10 instanced boxes and cylinders in 3-4 colours on a short strip; tarpaulin/parasol canopy slabs as sensors. Do not show fire or collapse.
- **go-slow:** a queue of high-poled buses, a lorry with a load of boxes, and keke pulled in nose-to-tail; hawkers weaving between vehicles (S2, S41); bridgehead choke point. Use a long block of box-vehicles at low speed, hazard-light blink in amber, a hand-painted "Go-slow" cone board. No police/bribe scenes.
- **keke-swerve:** a three-wheeled keke (box body, canopy slab, 3 cylinder wheels, forward lamp) cutting across lanes, pulling out of a keke rank at junctions and roundabouts (S7, S34); a warning chevron on the road; its path is a lateral slide with a body-roll tilt. Avoid yellow-green-white-red liveries; use a neutral palette (see section 12).
