# Katsina — world research dossier

Status: DRAFT (research only; not reviewed by a local reviewer). Every landmark stays `verified: false` until someone from the state signs off in section 14.

Rules that bind this dossier (AGENTS.md, docs/GUARDRAILS.md, docs/chunks/C27.md, C34.md, ADR-0009):
- No photos are downloaded, traced, copied or committed. Record a text description and the source URL only.
- A venue is one of: market, stadium, square, park, bridge, landmark, waterfront. A palace is scenery only, never a venue. No religious building as a landmark. Nothing named after a person. No recent disaster. No real politicians, parties, slogans or party colours.
- Welcome and farewell lines only in a language the state actually speaks.

House rule applied while writing (as in the Kwara and Borno dossiers): no real politician, ruler or official is named. A road, stadium, airport, university, square, market or park named after a person is called "person-named" and described by role. Source URLs whose slug contains a person's name are listed by publisher and gist only (marked URL withheld). Casualty figures are deliberately not recorded. Gate names are place names in daily use and are written as sources write them; where a gate is named for a historical person it is flagged P.

Evidence tags: **[2+]** two or more independent sources agree; **[1]** single source, treat as unconfirmed; **[inf]** my inference or general knowledge that no fetched source confirmed.

## 1. Scope and date
Researched 2026-10-27 by one agent using WebSearch (standard mode, plus a few extended) and WebFetch on: Wikipedia (Katsina city, Katsina State, Gates of Hausa kingdoms, Gobarau Minaret, Ajiwa Dam, Hausa architecture, the city stadium), Wikivoyage (Katsina, Hausa phrasebook), Wikimedia Commons category pages (file names only; no image opened or downloaded), Daily Trust, a travel blog, MileHacker, ConnectNigeria, Nigerian news and trade press, and design and architecture articles on Hausa facades. Sources for 2023-2026 incidents are search-result summaries and article text; many news aggregator pages carry misleading "2026" page-render dates, so event dates below are given only where an article's own text states them.

Honest headline: the text web is **thin on what Katsina's streets physically look like**. The best sources give names, materials, a few colours and Commons file names. Everything tagged `[inf]` is reasoning from those facts plus the Kano and Kaduna dossiers, not a sourced statement. I tried to look at real photos with the built-in browser, but the pane returned unrelated images (a Jos hillside, a flyover with no Katsina caption), so **no photo was viewed**; nothing here is a photo reading. Failed fetches: kupi.com (403), Britannica (403), archnet (429), Commons "Category:Katsina (city)" and "Category:Ajiwa Dam" (404), one national-news page (403).

Four findings change the draft pack materially:
1. **"Gobarau Minaret" is the minaret of a former mosque** (S5); it is rejected as a landmark, and this dossier also recommends not using it as scenery (section 9).
2. **The draft's stadium cannot be used.** There is no source for a "Katsina Township Stadium". The real city stadium opened in 2013 under another name, was renamed in 2016 after a 20th-century emir (person-named), and had a violence-and-ban episode in Nov 2025 and a player's death during a friendly in Aug 2026 (S7, S8). It is replaced by a flyover (bridge).
3. **Katsina's best visual difference from Kano is colour, not form:** the surviving city wall at Kofar Sauri is described as a tall red wall among zinc roofs (S9), the walls are "mud and burnt bricks" (S10), and the old college that is now the National Museum is red-baked clay (S10, S12). Kano's world is tan mud; Katsina's should be **rose-red laterite and brick, cream render, and a pale hot sky**.
4. **Insecurity is real across the state in 2025-2026** (rural LGAs, Funtua, Dutsin-Ma; a mosque attack in 2025). Nothing found at or near the five chosen venues in Katsina city, but the world must keep every rural segment generic (section 11).

## 3. Built form
Overall character: **a low, wide, dry-country Hausa walled city of about 140 km2 (S1)**, much of its old wall gone under new building (S9), with a rose-red clay and brick old core, cream-rendered decorated houses, flat-roofed shops, a red-brick colonial college, and a newer ring of GRA villas, plazas, underpasses and a flyover. Katsina is **not a copy of Kano**: Kano is described as tan mud with horn-cone corners (see the Kano dossier); the Katsina sources emphasise **red burnt brick and laterite, a long wall with gates, and decorated facades** (S9, S10, S15).

### 3a. Old and vernacular
- **Materials `[2+]`:** laterite is the main material (S15); houses are conical sun-dried bricks (tubali) in thick mud mortar, walls taper from about five bricks at the base to about two at the top (S15); plaster is clay-rich mud (kasa) often with red pigment, "giving houses their reddish-brown finish" (S15); swamp mud for plaster. City walls: "mud and burnt bricks", mostly rebuilt in brick and concrete, one mud section left (S10); the Kofar Sauri survivor is red (S9).
- **Roofs `[2]`:** flat roofs on azara (deleb palm) beams; vaulted and domed interiors in palaces; long drainage eaves; parapets and roof pinnacles (zankwaye) are Hausa-wide (S15; the Kano dossier S3). Modern houses add zinc sheets `[inf]`.
- **Entrance `[2+]`:** main door (kofa) of wood or iron, a zaure entrance room, then forecourt and private inner court (S15). Windows small, slit-like and high, with wooden shutters (S15). Decoration is concentrated on the entry and the street-facing wall (S15 and Mariam Issoufou article).
- **Facade ornament `[2+]`:** raised or incised motifs: the Dagi knot, a staff of office, a sword, interlacing arabesque, wall calligraphy (S15); later 20th-century motifs are smaller and interlaced (S15). Katsina and Kano are both cited for decorated facades (S15). Decorated traditional houses have become rarer since the 1970s because cement-block houses are the status symbol (Mariam Issoufou article). Cement plaster came with colonial building, creating the plasterer trade (mai shafe).
- **Gates `[2+]`:** the best-preserved gate keeps its original form in natural brown mud, renovated in 2007 (S3, S9). The others have been painted, rebuilt or lost; gates are never locked (S9). Gate form is **not described in any text** `[inf]`: a thick earthen pier pair, a round or pointed arch passage, a flat parapet, painted or decorated face with the Hausa emblem on some.
- **Wall `[1+inf]`:** at Kofar Sauri the one standing section is red, reported about 20 m high (S9; the curator says it "must have been very high" and has weathered lower, so treat 20 m as a [1] exaggeration and model about 8-10 m `[inf]`), surrounded by zinc-roofed dwellings; residents have cut laterite from it for new blocks (S9). Model as a **tall tapering red rampart with a gate arch, a depression at the foot, zinc roofs crowding against it**. No people urinating, no rubbish (the article says both; do not show).
- **Palace area (scenery only, not chosen):** the emir's palace is a very large compound in the old centre; gate with the Hausa emblem; "Blue Room" in one blog (S10, S12); the open field before it is the durbar ground (S10). Do not model the palace gate, emblem, horsemen or durbar (guardrail 11).
- **Mosque and minaret (not modelled):** the old central mosque and its 15 m mud tower sit in the old centre (S4, S5, S11). Do not model.

### 3b. Colonial and mid-century
- **Old teachers' college (now the National Museum) `[2+]`:** built 1921 (opened 1922 per one source) of red-baked mud and fired clay; the red brick is maintained; it has a gate with a step from which you can look over the rooftops; parts need renovation; the gate carries a word as inscription (S10, S12, S34). It is on the national monuments list (S12). Form beyond that is `[inf]`: long single-storey red-brick ranges with deep arcaded verandas, lime-washed window hoods, flat or low-pitched roofs, a central gate block. Model a **brick-red hall with a wide arch gate and two or three steps up**.
- **GRA / civic:** Government Reserve Area with a central roundabout (now a flyover) and an "old Government House" the emir visits on the second day of the durbar (S24, S18). Villas, a hotel and a mall are named in Commons files (S13). Form `[inf]`: tiled villas behind garden walls, wide avenues, colonial bungalows.
- **Markets and motor parks `[1]`:** a crowded bus garage with more passengers than buses (S10); "Kofar Kaura Market" (S13); neighbourhood markets (S11); a weekly women-majority market beside the general hospital (S17; skip: leprosy-linked origin).

### 3c. Contemporary city
- **Flyover and underpasses `[2+]`:** the state's first flyover is an interchange bridge at the GRA central roundabout (S18); two underpasses at Kofar Kaura and Kofar Kwaya (S18). Both underpasses flooded in 2023 (S19): the world should show them dry.
- **Plazas and commerce `[1+inf]`:** a "City Mall", hotels (several named in S4, S13: invent brands), restaurants on the main avenues (S4), banks and fuel stations `[inf]`. Model 2-4 storey cream or pale-grey rendered blocks with stacked fascias, grills and rooftop tanks, as in the Kano and Kaduna dossiers (S28).
- **Industry `[1]`:** a steel rolling mill (a roundabout is named after it, S14, S30), a flour mill and agro processing `[inf]`. Shed lots.
- **Drainage `[1]`:** a large drainage programme and open concrete drains after the 2023 flood (Gazette NG headline, URL withheld).

### 3d. Rural edge (keep generic)
Hamlets of flat-roofed clay compounds, zinc sheds, granaries `[inf]`; irrigated vegetable plots; millet and sorghum fields (S2, S25). **No herds, camps, uniforms, checkpoints or schools** (section 11).

## 4. Road
- **Surface `[inf]`:** grey asphalt on the main avenues with painted white edge lines and kerbed medians on the dual carriageways; red-brown laterite and sand shoulders; side streets pale sand or patched asphalt. The roundabout is the city's trademark: about 14 "Roundabout in Katsina City" files plus named ones (S14).
- **Roundabouts `[2+]`:** many; a Central Market roundabout, a Central Mosque roundabout and a Liyafa roundabout are named in 2023 enforcement reports (S20 and Gazette NG revenue-board report), a Kiddies roundabout, a Kwado roundabout, a WTC roundabout, a General Hospital roundabout and a steel-rolling-mill roundabout appear in file names (S14). Monuments in them are **not documented** (a Daily Trust list of monument roundabouts is about Kano); do not invent one.
- **Dual carriageway and bridges `[2+]`:** the GRA interchange (six lanes in one report, S18); two underpasses (S18); the A9 to Kano and A2 / A126 federal roads (S2); a ring road (S2).
- **Poles, wires, signs `[inf]`:** concrete poles with sagging wire bundles on side streets; tall curved-arm street lights on the avenues; green direction boards; billboards at roundabouts. A mast clock or sign pylon at the market roundabout is a safe generic prop.
- **Open drains `[inf]`:** covered concrete drains with slab crossings in front of shops (after the post-2023 programme, 3c).
- **Transport `[2+]`:** commercial motorcycles and tricycles are everywhere in northern cities; Katsina's state rules restrict them at night (about 10 pm to 6 am; S20), and revenue boards check riders at the central roundabouts (Gazette NG 2023). A **2026 executive order bans motorcycles in two rural LGAs** (S21) and cites bandit mobility: do **not** show motorcycles in rural segments. Taxis and minibuses `[1]` (S10); flatbed trucks `[inf]`.
- **Okada status `[2]`:** no outright okada ban in the metropolis found; night restriction only (S20); no source states day use explicitly. See section 12 for the ride decision.
- **Roadside `[1+inf]`:** hand carts and wheelbarrows, water carriers (Commons: "young Hausa man fetching water for people", S14), sugarcane seller (S13), fura da nono, zobo and kunun tsamiya sellers (S4), suya grillers, phone-charging and recharge-card kiosks, sack stacks at shopfronts `[inf]`.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- **Zone `[2+]`:** hot semi-arid steppe (Koppen BSh) in the city (S1), West Sudanian savanna with Sahelian transition in the north (S2); flat to gently undulating plain at about 500 m `[inf]`; no hills in the city (no hill source found; Funtua has one but is far). Katsina is **flat**: the skyline is walls, towers of water and flyovers, not a hill.
- **Rainfall `[2, conflicting]`:** about 600 mm a year in the city (S1, July-August peak), about 790 mm for the state (S2), 800-1,000 mm in a travel guide (S26). Rainy season June-September (S26).
- **Trees `[partly sourced]`:** Faidherbia (Commons file "Faidherbia tree in the dry Katsina region", S13) is a field tree that is **leafless in the rains and leafy in the dry season** `[inf, general knowledge]`: leaf-state matters for a November world, so draw it leafy; neem (Commons, S13); a sapotaceous tree (shea-like, S13); baobab, tamarind, locust-bean, dum and date palms `[inf: Kano dossier and regional ecology; no Katsina source found]`; wild Leptadenia shrub (S13).
- **Crops `[2+]`:** millet, sorghum, maize, groundnut, cotton (S2); irrigated tomatoes starting in November, onions, potatoes (Bakori), soybean, wheat (S25). In November expect **harvested millet and sorghum stubble, stacked stalks, and irrigated tomato and onion plots on the dam edge**.
- **Soil `[inf]`:** red-brown ferruginous sand (laterite), pale sandy edges; the Kano world uses `#B8895A` ground, so Katsina uses a pinker, greyer red-sand.
- **Water `[2]`:** seasonal streams (Gada) and a few dams; **Ajiwa Dam** about 16 km east-south-east of the city supplies drinking water and irrigation (S6); Commons lists green planted areas and a "natural lake" there (S13). Nearly no perennial water in the city.
- **Wildlife/livestock:** a camel appears in a Commons file (S13); cattle, goats and sheep are widely reared (S2). **Do not model herds, herders or cattle trails** (section 6 avoid list).
- **Climate and light `[2+]`:**
  - **Harmattan, mid-Nov to Feb:** dust haze, reduced visibility, flight delays (S26); coolest Dec-Jan; days about 20-35 C (S26).
  - **Late dry, Mar-May:** hottest, April about 40 C (S1, S26).
  - **Rains, June-Sept:** green fields, heavy downpours that flood underpasses (S19).
