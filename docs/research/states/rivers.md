# Rivers (capital Port Harcourt) — world research dossier

Status: DRAFT (research only; not reviewed by a local reviewer). Every landmark stays `verified: false` until someone from the state signs off in section 14.

Rules that bind this dossier (AGENTS.md, docs/GUARDRAILS.md, docs/chunks/C27.md, C34.md, ADR-0009):
- No photos are downloaded, traced, copied or committed. Record a text description and the source URL only.
- A venue is one of: market, stadium, square, park, bridge, landmark, waterfront. A palace is scenery only, never a venue. No religious building as a landmark. Nothing named after a person. No recent disaster. No real politicians, parties, slogans or party colours.
- Welcome and farewell lines only in a language the state actually speaks.

Notation: **(2S)** = two or more independent sources agree. **(1S)** = single source, treat as unconfirmed. **(INF)** = my modelling inference or general knowledge, no source found. Real people are deliberately not named in this file (AGENTS.md); where a source names one, I describe the thing instead.

## 1. Scope and date
- Researched 2026-10-27 for the Rivers pack (`creek-delta` kit, `ss-brass`). Routes: WebSearch (standard, extended for thin topics), WebFetch of Wikipedia, Commons file pages/categories, planning and heritage papers, news and travel writing. No browser tools, no downloads, no other file touched.
- Honest limits: the fetch tool returns summaries, not raw pages, so many pages gave text facts but almost no visual description. Several pages returned 403/TLS errors (Guardian NG, two academic hosts) and one run of searches failed on a transient tool error and was retried. Commons pages gave file names and captions, not scene descriptions; I treat them as (1S) hints at best.
- Two things in the current pack could not be found anywhere and are flagged for removal: "Amadi Bridge" and "Garden City Park" (section 9).
- `signatureRide: "danfo"` in the current pack is a Lagos bus. Rivers sources describe kekes (tricycles), minibuses, shared "drop" cabs and a restriction on okada in much of the city. Recommend `keke` (matches the keke-swerve hazard and the Delta/Bayelsa packs).
- Rivers must not read as Delta/Bayelsa re-skins (both also `creek-delta` with stilt houses and mangrove). Rivers' differentiators are the planned colonial grid and tree-lined GRA, a big contemporary oil-city (flyovers, towers, malls), the Creek Road working waterfront, and the Bonny estuary lighthouse.

## 3. Built form

### 3a. Waterfront and creek settlements (vernacular, informal)
- Port Harcourt calls its low-income waterside districts "watersides" (7, 8). Fabric: self-built from concrete block, timber and corrugated sheet (6) (2S with 8: mud-built rooms, makeshift shops). Ground is raised by filling the creek edge with black mangrove mud and refuse (6, 8) (2S), so many houses stand on stilts or short piles at the water edge (8 says "makeshift stilt houses").
- Ijaw vernacular in the creeks: wood-and-mud walls, thatch or corrugated zinc roofs, stilts over swamp, narrow plank walkways, canoes as street (41, loose summaries) (INF for heights).
- Modelling guess (INF): rectangular plank box 4x6 m, one storey on posts 1.2-2.5 m high, low gable or single-pitch zinc roof, one door onto a plank jetty, 1-2 small shuttered windows, smoke-blackened lean-to for fish drying. Planks weathered grey-brown, roofs rust-red to dull silver, a few painted doors (pale blue, mint, ochre).
- Key difference from Delta/Bayelsa: here the stilts sit directly beside tarmac and concrete (Diobu/Borokiri waterfront, Creek Road) with oil-store tanks behind (a photo caption in 6/e-architect mentions rooftops with oil stores behind) rather than deep inside empty mangrove.
- Do NOT depict demolition, rubble, evictions or fire: these settlements have been the subject of serious news coverage; the game shows only living, working waterfront.
- Canoe houses: Okrika kin groups are "war canoe houses" (a social unit, not a building) (47 via Okrika wiki, 1S) so do not model them as boats.
- Bonny: houses of palm leaf and branches exist in one Bonny settlement (20, 2S, same text reused). Travel copy claims 1800s stone houses at Okoloama (1S, promotional): treat as unconfirmed.

### 3b. Colonial layer
- Port Harcourt was founded 1912-13 as a coal port linked by rail to Enugu (1, 4, 36 context) (2S). Land taken from Ikwerre and Okrika (7).
- Planned "European quarter" (now Old GRA) had wide curving avenues and low-density good-quality housing in gardens (search summary of an academic abstract, 1S; 4 agrees on "neat colonial planning and extensive gardens") (2S loosely). The 1960s-70s Diobu variant of the colonial standard was a U-shaped bungalow (search summary, 1S).
- Bungalow look (general colonial type): single storey, deep verandah, steep pitched roof for rain (Port Harcourt architecture notes say pitched/gable roofs prevail because of heavy rain, 1S). Recent Old GRA is a mix of renovated bungalows, 1980s retro-mansions and new estates (search summary, 1S). Walls cream/pale ochre, roofs red-brown or green-grey corrugated (INF).
- Port Harcourt Golf Club in Old GRA dates from 1928 (38, 1S) so the old layout has green open land.

### 3c. Contemporary Port Harcourt
- Oil money brought overpasses, city blocks and taller buildings (1). An 18-storey secretariat "Point Block" is the tallest building in the South-East and South-South together (1, 1S; another source confirms at least 13 storeys because a ministry sits on the 13th floor).
- Common types (INF, consistent with 3, 10): 2-3 storey plastered concrete-block shops with flat or low hipped roofs, upper balconies with burglar-proof grilles, hand-painted boards; microapartment blocks (1); unfinished multi-storey concrete frames with zinc and nylon over openings (3); gated glazed offices and hotels in New GRA/Trans-Amadi; malls and a cinema (14, 1).
- Trans-Amadi: industrial estate with plants for glass bottles, tyres, aluminium and paper (38); also nightlife and restaurants (9, weak). Eleme has two refineries (1).
- Pleasure Park: built 2017 on a former borrow pit, with cinema, restaurants and mini-golf (14 and 2 more listings) (2S).
- Wall colours to use: cream, pale grey, terracotta, teal, deep blue gate panels, white parapets; avoid party colours (see 6).

### 3d. Markets
- Mile One (Diobu, Ikwerre Road): tables side by side selling identical goods, wares spread on walkways, hawkers weaving through traffic, no traffic lights/zebra crossings, incomplete buildings beside it (2, 3). Goods: second-hand clothes (okrika), shoes, fabrics, spices, dried fish, vegetables (9, plus a clothing-market guide) (2S for okrika/fabrics).
- Look (INF): low concrete shed halls with corrugated roofs and gable ends, outer ring of tables under large striped umbrellas and tarpaulins, stacked cartons, rails of clothes, hand-painted signs.
- Creek Road Market (Borokiri, Old Township): yam trucks arrive almost daily, youths offload, cart pushers; live chickens, goats, sheep (11); seafood (fish, prawns, crabs), uneven wet floor (12); tight stalls and boats unloading at the Bonny jetty (13) (3S).

## 4. Road
- Surface: major roads are dual carriageway; state specs cite 7.6 m lanes with streetlights (search summary, Ahoada-Omoku) and an asphalt wearing course with 2.75 m outer shoulders (East-West Road) (1S each, not PH streets). Surface colour dark grey, wet-black in rain; patchy and potholed on side streets (10: "rough streets"; 36). Kerbs: raised concrete kerbs in GRA and new roads; uneven or "vanishing" pavements elsewhere (9, 1S weak) (INF).
- Median and flyovers: PH has several flyovers that are not full interchanges, with at-grade roundabouts under them (Garrison/Rebisi, Rumuokoro/Okoro-nu-odu, Rumuogba/Artillery, Eleme Junction, Eliozu, a Rumuola flyover and a new roundabout planned) (36, 2S). Rumuokoro has a roadside market that adds to jams (36).
- Roundabouts: yes, many; also "Agip roundabout" appears as a Commons file title (40, 1S).
- Shoulders: mud and standing water after rain (INF; 35 for flooding). Laterite/orange-brown soil exposed where verges are cut (INF; soils not sourced).
- Drainage: open concrete channels often blocked (35, 2S). Show as slab-covered drains along the kerb.
- Roadside trade: bole (roasted plantain) and fish stands, roasted corn, grills glowing at night along Aba Road (10 and 9; 34) (2S); smoked cow-skin snacks (24); phone-charging, fruit baskets and school materials (3).
- Poles and wires: wires hang low over traders; concrete poles break and lean (Port Harcourt news on low-hanging cables, a market pole resting on a shop roof, and another city's poles; search summaries) (1S for PH). Use concrete poles with cross-arms and drooping cable.
- Transport: keke tricycles are the most common short trip; okada restricted or banned in much of the city; minibuses and shared "drop" cabs (9 and a second travel blog, 2S). Colour of kekes: NOT FOUND, so do not claim a state colour (open question).
- Water crossings: Bonny, Okrika and creek crossings exist; Okrika-Borikiri 3.75 km road with three bridges (Kolabi 180 m, Abotoru 1,120 m, Okpoka 490 m) has been stalled since 2012, current status unclear (37, 2S). The Bodo-Bonny road with a 502 m creek bridge (search summary) is very recent and politically credited, so it is avoided.
- Signs: green/white blue-ish direction gantries with junction names (INF); hand-painted trade boards (a museum record shows hand-painted barber boards in Nigeria, 1S).

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- Terrain: low-lying flat coastal plain, tidal creeks, wetlands, reclaimed land (21, 43) (2S). Zones: freshwater swamp, mangrove swamp, coastal sand ridges (21).
- Mangroves: dense mangrove covers Bonny outside its southern edge (NLE master plan summary; 20) (2S). Rhizophora with arched stilt roots standing in dark mud (INF).
- Nipa palm: fronds to ~9 m rising straight from mud, trunk hidden (22); spreads where mangrove was cut, forms dense single-species stands (23), leaves used for roofing and baskets (22, 23) (2S). Shape for modelling: a fan of long upright pinnate fronds, no visible trunk.
- Oil palm: the dominant vegetation in most inland areas (21). Raffia is used to wrap gifts in Ikwerre custom (25, 1S) and as building material (41, 1S); treat raffia palm as swampy-edge accent (INF).
- Crops/trade goods: cassava (a cassava farm near Abonnema wharf appears as a Commons title, 40; also Okrika trade in cassava, plantain, yam, oil palm produce per Britannica via search summary) (2S); plantain/bole (34).
- Trees in town: "Garden City" with trees and flowers on street corners and pavements (10 and 9, 2S); leafy lawns in Old GRA (9). Species not sourced; use mango-like round crowns and flame-tree red/orange accent only as small flowers (avoid solid red masses, see 6).
- Water: red-brown and murky in creeks (INF); Commons title "Traditional wooden canoes docked along the muddy river banks" (40, 1S). Mud banks, black mud near mangrove (6).
- Climate: heavy seasonal rain Mar-Nov with a July peak, humidity 90-100%, 25-28 C (21, 1) (2S). Dry season Dec-Feb (1). Harmattan "haze" in Dec-Jan claimed by 9 but Wikipedia says harmattan is weak (1): use a faint pale haze only in the dry-season variant.
- Light: overcast bright grey-white sky, humid soft haze, sudden downpours (9, 10). Gas flares: gas burns at a petrochemical plant in Port Harcourt (24) and soot is reported in Abuloma, Iwofe, Rupokwu, Okrika, Woji (1) (2S). For the game: only a distant slim stack with a small orange flame and a faint glow in the sky at the horizon; no smoke plumes over people, no spills, no pollution scenes.

## 6. Dress
Men:
- Everyday: short or long-sleeved shirts, T-shirts, trousers or jeans, sandals, canvas shoes, football shirts, plain caps (INF; no source found for everyday PH street dress).
- Cultural: a George wrapper (called njiri by the Ijaw) tied at the waist with an etibo/owoko top (a long collarless button-front shirt, round neck, side pocket), coral beads and walking stick (25, 26, 27, 28) (4S, with Ikwerre "Jioji" wrapper under 25). Itsekiri (neighbour in Delta) men add a one-feather hat (search summary, outside Rivers).
Women:
- Wrapper in two pieces with a fitted blouse and matching head-tie (25, 26, 28) (3S); layered wrappers of India/Injiri/Krukru Bite cloth, madras, damask, velvet, lace blouse, coral beads, beaded hats/crowns (26, 28) (2S). Everyday: wrapper and blouse, ankara skirt-and-top, head-tie, sandals, a baby tied with a wrapper (INF; a wrapper doubles as a baby sling per the Wrapper entry).
Children: school uniforms (plaid dress or shirt-and-shorts), flip-flops (INF).
Working people (INF unless stated): fisherfolk in rolled trousers/shorts and singlets or old shirts, bare-footed or boots, paddling dugouts, throwing cast nets; fish sellers in wrappers with basins on the head; market porters with cart pushers in sleeveless tops; keke/bus drivers in short sleeves; bole sellers in aprons by a charcoal drum; yam-truck offloaders (11) in vests.
Accessories: coral-bead necklaces, beaded hats, canoe-shaped hats (Kalabari canoe hat is mentioned in a search snippet, 1S), hand fans.

**Avoid list**
- A complete etibo shirt with a matching hat and walking stick as a head-to-toe look: source 28 says this style is associated with a former national leader (not named here). Per AGENTS.md that is a signature look. Use the shirt or wrapper separately, hatless, and not in one heroic outfit.
- Any head-to-toe red, white and green or green-and-white combination; plain red or green caps; branded-looking "campaign" caps and shirts; any straight primary-colour block that matches a registered party's colours. Use teal, ochre, indigo, maroon-brown, mustard.
- A plain bright red or bright green fila/cap on a lead or villain figure; a red beaded cap with white feather (chieftaincy regalia) - chiefly dress is also a palace-adjacent cue.
- Bare-breasted initiation or festival dress (Iria is described in the Okrika wiki article): not shown.
- Military/police uniforms, weapons, camouflage (no weapons or insecurity per GUARDRAILS 3).
- Soot-stained clothes as a pollution message.

## 7. Signs and language
- English dominates formal signs, with Nigerian Pidgin in informal speech and humour. A Pidgin sign vocabulary exists: "DEY OPEN", "DON KLOS", "KOMOT", "MAN-DEM", "WOMAN-DEM", "DEM FORBID AM" (31, 1S for sign forms, but Pidgin forms themselves 2S with 32).
- Words (2S): abeg (please), wetin (what), wahala (trouble), oga (boss), sabi (know), una (you plural), dey, make we, how far. Port Harcourt Pidgin is a documented variety (32 and an APICS summary).
- Trade words: bole (roasted plantain), fish, okrika (second-hand clothes, 2S), "Mile 1/Mile 3", "Creek Road", "keke", "drop" (shared cab), "buka" and "mama put" (INF, unverified for PH).
- Business-name style: "... Enterprises", "... Ventures", "... Global Ltd", "God's Time", "Grace" and place names such as "Garden City" (INF; a sociolinguistics paper confirms Nigerian business names are a heavy identity marker but concerned Lagos, 1S).
- Sample board text (all original, safe): "BOLE & FISH - HOT HOT", "FRESH FISH - CREEK ROAD", "OKRIKA CLOTHES - ALL SIZE", "DEY OPEN", "GOD DEY - PHARMACY", "BARBING SALON", "KEKE PARK - ABEG PARK WELL", "WELCOME TO GARDEN CITY".
- Local-language words (all need a native speaker; confidence in section 10): Izon "nụ́a" = thanks/welcome (30, 29) (2S, different dialect than Kalabari); Izon "dibuomu" = safe journey (29, 1S); Ikwerre greetings "Iwhiala?" (morning, from a woman to men), "Chibo" (night), "Nderiya mma!" ### The best five (and why)
1. **Mile One Market** (market): best-documented (2, 3, 9, 8), unmistakable street energy, easy primitives; one slot for the dense Diobu district.
2. **Creek Road Waterfront** (waterfront): the only place where the planned city, the creek and boats meet; documented by four sources (11, 12, 13, 9); sets up stilts and canoes without copying Delta/Bayelsa.
3. **Port Harcourt Pleasure Park** (park): the contemporary-city landmark, clean rule record, documented by Wikipedia and listings (14); gives a bright civic scene that is not water.
4. **Garrison Flyover** (bridge): replaces the unverifiable "Amadi Bridge". The flyover-over-roundabout is a real, distinct PH form (36), different from Delta's Niger Bridge. Name needs local confirmation.
5. **Bonny River (Field Point) Lighthouse** (landmark): unique to Rivers, a striking silhouette, documented by three listings (17, 18). It also replaces the stadium slot: the only stadium that is notable has been renamed after a person (see section 9).

## 11. Hazard visual notes
- **flood-sheet**: A thin sheet of shallow brown (#8A6B4A at 55% alpha) water across one or two lanes, with a lighter reflective strip and expanding ring ripples as the car passes; blocked drain slabs and a few plastic basins/stools at the kerb. Telegraph with a small hand-painted "WATER" board or an upturned bucket.
- **keke-swerve**: A roofed three-wheeler (box body, round-nosed cab, single front wheel, open sides, roof tarp, driver without door) edges out from a kerbside stop and swerves two lanes; pre-tell with an indicator blink and a lean. Colour of Rivers kekes not sourced: use two non-party tones (mustard and teal) and no tinted panels.
- **go-slow**: A queue of minibuses and cars with tail lights, a keke weaving along the shoulder, hawkers walking between cars with baskets and phone chargers (3: hawkers weave through traffic), a flyover shadow above and a roadside market cluster (36).
