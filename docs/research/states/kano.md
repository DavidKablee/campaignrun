## 3. Built form
Scale: Kano is a "vast, low, flat city" ringed by a wall; roofline broken only by a few minarets and some taller blocks in newer northern quarters [S19, low reliability]; city on a plain ~480 m above sea level with Dala and Goron Dutse hills inside [S8, S9] [2+].

### Old / vernacular (the "Birni", inside the wall)
- **Material.** Earth: tubali (small cone-shaped sun-dried mud bricks), rammed/puddled earth, laid in mud mortar; earth dug from nearby borrow pits [S3, S4, S9] [2+]. Straw, husks, dung added [search summary, 1]. Wall plaster is mud mixed with locust-bean-pod liquid (makuba) and potash [S4; Gidan Makama museum label summary] [2]. New walls look crisp; rain makes them slump into soft, rounded forms; owners repair every dry season [S1] [1].
- **Colour.** Plain earth reads warm tan to red-brown (laterite) [S1, S9] [2+]; the city wall is "mud and sand" [S7, S9] [2]. Decoration on palaces and rich houses is described as "colourful" but no source names the colours [S2, S3] -> pick from the dye palette in section 12 and flag it. Women are said to paint and relief-mould house exteriors [search summary, 1].
- **Form.** Inward-looking rectangular compounds: tall perimeter wall with few small openings, rooms around one or more courtyards [S1, S3] [2]. Roofs are flat and parapeted in dry Kano/Katsina/Sokoto (domes where rain is heavier) [S3; Moughtin-based summaries] [2]; azara palm beams with split-palm herringbone and mud topping [S4 + search summary] [2]. Houses are 1 storey, occasionally a raised roof room; wealthy ones have a roof terrace [G].
- **Zaure (entrance hall).** A gatehouse room that is the first room a visitor meets, used to receive male guests without entering the house [S3 + Archnet summary of the palace gate] [2]. Outer doors are wooden or iron, hung on pivots, set in a deep thick arch/lintel; inner doors are often only grass curtains [S4] [1]. Model as: box mass projecting from the wall, dark rectangular doorway with slightly rounded top, a bench (dakali) beside it [G for bench].
- **Zankwaye (parapet horns).** Reinforced vertical pinnacles at roof corners and along the parapet, "like the horns of a bull"; origin as builders' handholds [S2, S3] [2, likely same origin]. Silhouette = flat roof with a cone/pyramid horn at each corner and sometimes a row of smaller ones over the zaure.
- **Relief ornament.** Freehand-engraved geometric patterns, calligraphy, slashes, punctures and arch motifs on facades; richer = wealthier owner [S1, S2, S3] [2+]. No motif names verified.
- **Heights and windows.** Windows are small openings high on the wall on the sheltered side, sometimes lattice screens [S4] [1]. Heights otherwise not sourced.
- **City wall and gates.** Walls once ~10 miles long and ~30 ft high [1851 account, S8]; today ~10-11 m high, ~9 m thick, gates ~9 m wide [S6]; other sources say ~15 m high, ~12 m base [S5]: **sources disagree, use 10 m high as the model height and make the base wide and sloping** [2]. Only 25-30% still stands; houses are built on and against it; the old ditch has become ponds; some gates were renovated (2004, 2014) and repainted [S6, S7] [2]. **Gate count: 15 named [S5, S6]** (one guide says ~18 [search summary]). Gate shape is **not described in any text I could reach**; model as a chunk of wall with a tall round-topped or rectangular passage and crenellated top, flagged as inferred.
- **Kurmi market (old city).** Dense lanes, "labyrinth" (1851 account [S8]; a travel guide uses the same word) [2]; tight dirt alleys shaded by corrugated sheets, handcarts, women with calabashes, tailors on pedal machines, ~16 ha [S11] [1]. Early market had bamboo stalls in rows; the 1909 rebuild had 755 clay stalls [S10] [1].
- **Dye pits (Kofar Mata).** A walled compound of deep circular pits dug into the ground, each holding indigo; dyers crouch over pits; cloth hung to dry and oxidise, shades from sky blue to near black; near the mud wall [S13, S14, S15] [2+]. Pit count: "more than 100" / "125" / "~100", most disused and clogged [S14, S15] [2]. Entrance sign dates the site to 1498 [S14, S15] [2].

### Colonial and mid-century
- **Sabon Gari.** Planned outside the wall from about 1911, a regular grid contrasted with the old-city maze; colonial planning memo of 1923 and a 1914 segregation proclamation [S27] [2]. Settled by southern migrants; streets carry southern names (a Commons file title mentions an "Ibom road" in Sabon Gari) [S41, 1]. Colonial-era building used cement, concrete and corrugated iron [S27, 1]. Shopfronts there: provision stores, barber shops, medicine stores, gas-refill shops, and a busy Bata-area junction with cars, buses and tricycles (Guardian NG-style report, 1). Look of the buildings is **[G]**: single and two-storey rendered concrete blocks, shop verandas with columns, louvred windows, hipped or gable corrugated roofs.
- **Rail and groundnut era.** Railway reached Kano 1911 [S8]. Groundnut pyramids: stepped/pyramid stacks of up to ~15,000 sacks, at a depot at Kofar Nassarawa; gone by the 1960s-80s, revival effort 2014 [S18] [2 accounts, dates conflict]. Colour and covering unstated.
- **Colonial governor's house** now a museum (1900s) [S7, 1]. Palace and museum buildings are scenery only.

### Contemporary
- Concrete and steel houses with zinc or aluminium roofs are seen as the "progress" look [S33] [2]; plastered sandcrete blocks, perimeter walls, steel gates [G].
- **Zoo Road / Tarauni:** shopping plazas, banks (several named banks on Zoo Road), a very large mall (31,000 m2 built area, announced 2012; opening status unverified), the Trade Fair Complex and the zoo [S22, S23] [2+]. Physical look beyond "modern shopping complex" is **[G]**: 2-5 storey concrete plazas, glazed or cladded fronts, big painted signboards.
- **Bridges/flyovers:** Silver Jubilee flyover at Kofar Nassarawa, a Kofar Kabuga underpass, a Kofar Ruwa underpass, several 2013-2015 flyover projects [S8; Commons file title for 2013 construction] [2]. Flyovers are politically debated in Kano press (so keep them as road furniture, not as heroes).
- **Skyline:** a few mid-rise blocks in Bompai/Nassarawa GRA/Zoo Road; no glass-tower skyline found [search negative]. Do not draw towers above ~6 storeys.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- **Biome.** Sudanian savanna south of the Sahel [S8]. Rainfall ~900-1,150 mm, almost all June-September [S8, S17] [2]. Dec-Feb mornings 14-16 C [S8, 1].
- **Trees.** Scattered *Faidherbia albida* (Hausa *gawo*) in farmland, in decline on Kano farms [S39] [1-2]; acacias dominate the Sahel and baobab/doum palm are characteristic regionally [S40, regional, 1]. **Neem, dum palm and mango in town: [G]**, widely expected as shade/street trees, not confirmed by a source. Trees are sparse and low; no forest.
- **Crops.** Millet, cowpeas, sorghum, maize, rice for food; groundnuts and cotton for sale; also sesame, soy, garlic, chili [S17] [2]. Millet/groundnut rotation among farm trees [S39 WOCAT, region unclear, 1]. In Nov-Dec fields are harvested stubble and bare soil [G].
- **Terrain.** Flat plain; two rock hills inside the city: Dala (~534 m above sea level, a bare red laterite outcrop, perhaps ~50-60 m above the plain) and Goron Dutse (~517 m) [S9 + S19 low reliability] [2 for names/heights, 1 for the 60 m relief]. A stair of 101 (or 999) steps is reported [sources conflict]. Part of Dala was dug out as an underground water tank [S7, 1].
- **Water.** Kano and Challawa rivers join near the city; lowland pools and borrow pits near the old walls; dye pits are not natural water [S8, S9] [2].
- **Soil.** Sandy tan to laterite red-brown [S1, S19] [2].
- **Light and sky.** Harmattan runs Nov-Mar, peak mid-Jan; Kano is named repeatedly for dust-haze visibility below 1,000 m, worst before dawn and at night [S29] [2+]. Colour: yellow to brown haze, sun dimmed or reddish, fine dust on every surface [S29: NASA, press] [2+]. No source gives a "white sky"; use pale ochre. Dec temperatures cool; people wear head cloths and nose masks against dust and woolly habar kada caps against cold [S29 VON, S35] [2].

**Avoid list**
- The **red cap** tied to a Kano political movement [S37, 2]: no red caps at all, and no red hula. Avoid any cap or turban in solid, saturated red, and avoid the "long red cap" worn by palace staff [S35].
- Party colour sets: do not combine green + white, green + white + red, white + blue + red or bright orange + any of those on one character, banner or building; keep flags off poles entirely. No brooms, umbrellas, or other well-known party emblem shapes as props; no party-style sash.
- No emir/palace costume (tall red cap, rawani with royal regalia, horsemen in chain mail): palaces are scenery only and royal dress implies endorsement.
- No almajiri-style alms-bowl or begging scenes, no Quranic-school children, no prayer scenes (religious-joke and poverty-stereotype risk).
- No weapons, no swords or lances (durbar), no dambe boxers, no security uniforms or checkpoints.
- No face veil/niqab as a gag; hijab and mayafi shown neutrally and in varied colours.

**Chosen five and why**
1. **Kurmi Market** (market): the most recognisable place, strong built-form identity (earthen gate, tin lanes), pack already uses it.
2. **Kofar Mata Dye Pits** (landmark): colour identity (indigo) no other state has; circles are cheap to model.
3. **Dala Hill** (landmark): a single silhouette that tells the player "Kano" from a distance; no person, religion or palace.
4. **Kano Race Course** (stadium): replaces the pack's vague "Race Course Ground" with a verified [2] place; horse racing is distinctive; the only stadium-class venue that is not person-named.
5. **Zoo Road Trade Fair Complex** (landmark): carries the contemporary district. **Reserve if the reviewer rejects it:** Kofar Nassarawa Bridge, then Dawanau.

Why not a bridge: the pack's "Challawa Bridge" could not be found; the best real bridge candidate carries political baggage and is known only from one stock caption.

## 11. Hazard visual notes
- **dust-plume.** Harmattan dust is the state's real look [S29]. Cheap: translucent tan billboard quads trailing from trucks, keke and sand edges; a low-lying fog band raised in dust zones; a fog far-plane pull-in (e.g. 60 m) with pale ochre fog colour; soft sand-drift decals on road edges. Place plumes at the Dawanau/farm edge and the open sand shoulders. No injury, no vehicle flipped.
- **cart-weave.** Two-wheeled hand carts (box + 2 cylinder wheels + 2 handles) stacked with sacks, cloth bundles or tyres, pushed across lanes in markets and Sabon Gari; oscillate sideways on a sine path with a visible lean. Model the stack as 3-4 cubes. Carts at Kurmi confirmed [S11, 1]; donkey carts [G, avoid unless confirmed].
- **go-slow.** Nigerian word for a traffic jam. Show a tight queue of yellow keke, minibuses and flatbed trucks with hazard lights, hawkers walking between lanes with trays and water bags, brake-light red glow (a light red, not a party red). Keke are ~8 in 10 vehicles [S30], so make most of the queue keke. Best at the flyover approach and on Zoo Road. No arrests, no enforcement officers, no drivers fighting.
