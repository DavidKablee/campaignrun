## 1. Scope and date
Researched 2026-10-27 by one agent using WebSearch (standard mode throughout) and WebFetch on: Wikipedia (Ilorin, Kwara State, Sobi Hill, Owu Waterfalls, Kwara State Stadium, Jebba, Jebba Railway Bridge, Offa, Omu-Aran, Esie Museum, Ilorin Emirate, Yoruba architecture, Yoruba language, Aso oke, Fashion in Nigeria), Wikivoyage (Ilorin, Kwara, Yoruba phrasebook), Wikimedia Commons category and file pages (file names, captions and categories only; no image opened or downloaded), travel blogs, Nigerian news and trade press, and university abstracts.

Honest headline: the text web is **very thin on what Ilorin's old and new streets physically look like**. The best sources give names, materials in passing, layout facts and Commons file names. Everything tagged `[inf]` is reasoning from those facts plus general Nigerian built form, not a sourced statement. A Nupe-architecture page, the Owu Falls Wikipedia alias, the Ilorin Central Mosque page, one state-government road page and a Kwara tourism-board page all failed (404, 403 or DNS); for those only search-result summaries were seen and are tagged accordingly. The search tool is US-only; Nigerian coverage is patchy and many news pages carry misleading "2026" page-render dates, so incident dates below are given only where an article's text states them.

Two findings change the pack materially:
1. **Owu Falls cannot be a venue**: it is remote (unpaved drive, then about a 30-minute hike), its height is reported inconsistently (about 100 m vs 120 m), and 2026 reports place kidnappings on the Owu Falls access road and in the surrounding Isin and Ifelodun communities (section 9).
2. **"Oja Oba Market" is the Emir's market**: the name means "king's market", a university study calls it the Emir's Market, and a 1951 Commons record places the market in front of a gate of the emir's palace. Palace-linked, so rejected (section 9).

## 3. Built form
Overall character: **a city of about 760 km2 that grew by accretion round an old walled core** (S1, S39). It is Yoruba in population and language, Fulani-led in its emirate institutions, with Hausa, Nupe, Baatonu and Kanuri quarters [2+: S2, S9, S39]. Ilorin has compounds named for Yoruba towns (Offa, Oyo, Ijesha) beside compounds belonging to Fulbe, Kanuri, Gobir and Nupe groups [1: a Guardian Nigeria letter, search summary]. Identity is carried by language and religion more than ethnicity [1: S39]. Players should feel **an organic, mixed grain of low plastered compounds, zinc roofs, pottery and cloth, not a planned grid**.

### 3a. Old and vernacular (the signature blend, thinly sourced)
- **Yoruba courtyard compound `[2+]`:** rectangular compound, outer walls formed by contiguous rows of rooms, an inner courtyard for cooking and social life, veranda ring (S23). Earlier mud-brick and thatch; now concrete-block or cement-washed walls roofed in tin/zinc (S23, S25).
- **Nupe compound vocabulary `[1]`:** entrance hut, sleeping rooms, courtyard, animal pen, granaries, kitchen (S24). Gives a **front entrance room** that matches the Hausa zaure idea (see the Kano and Kaduna dossiers).
- **Ilorin-specific materials `[1]`:** mud (amo), cow dung (boto), straw (koriko), palm fronds (imo-ope) were the traditional Ilorin Emirate inputs (S25). Ancient clay houses stand beside modern houses in the city (S26). Dilapidated mud buildings are named in Commons files for Oke Onigbin (S11).
- **Modelling reading `[inf]`:** the "blend" the angle asks for is best shown as a **plastered earth-coloured block with a flat parapeted front (Hausa-side zaure/entrance room, no horn pinnacles because those read as Kano) behind which sits a low-pitched zinc gable range with a veranda on posts (Yoruba side)**. No source documents this exact Ilorin hybrid. Flat parapeted roofs in Ilorin compounds are `[inf]`, not sourced for Ilorin; reviewer must confirm (section 13).
- **Palace area (scenery only, not chosen):** the emir's palace is a complex of structures from different eras [1], with a main entrance and gates, "sculpted wooden poles in front of gate" [1: S13 file name], and a 1951 market with a town well in front of a palace gate [1: S15]. Do not model the palace, its gate poles or the nearby central mosque domes and minarets (section 9).
- **Pottery quarter `[2]`:** Dada/Okelele is "an acre of dust and terracotta" [S26], pots stacked into a wall of pots [S26], pieces left outside with no storage, shade under an old tree, a mud house [S27]. Firing is on open fires with smouldering heaps rather than a built kiln [S26 vs "crude kiln" S27: the two sources disagree; model an open fire mound]. Streets in the pottery wards are narrow and steep paths turn muddy in the rains [1: S26].
- **Weaving `[2]`:** aso-oke/ofi weaving on horizontal looms, men on narrow looms [S38, S3, S16]; modelled as a low open shed with a loom frame and hanging strips.

### 3b. Colonial and mid-century
- Rail came c. 1909-1912 on the Lagos-Kano line [1: S46]; Ilorin has a train station and the line still runs, described as slow and unreliable [S4, S13 file names]. Station form is **not described by any source** `[inf]`: long low rendered building, deep canopy on iron posts.
- A general post office, federal secretariat, local-government secretariat, bank branch, fire station and hospital building appear in Commons file names [S13] but with no description. Model as plain 1960s-80s civic blocks: sandcrete frame, window hoods, flat roof, painted cream or grey `[inf]`.
- Brazilian-style stuccoed storey houses are documented for Lagos, not Ilorin [S23]; do not assume them here.

### 3c. Contemporary city
- **Tanke / university axis `[2]`:** the area with the most listed places to eat, shop and stay [S3]; a reinforced-concrete flyover with service lanes is the newest big structure there [S34]. A mall, a hotel, bank branches and a fast-food outlet appear in file names [S13]; use invented brand names.
- **Government reserved area (GRA):** hotels and some restaurants [S3]. Model as tiled-villa and colonial-bungalow lots behind garden walls `[inf]`.
- **Industry `[2]`:** cashew processing (100 t a day, 2,000 jobs, S1), flour mill, pharma and food plants, motorcycle assembly [S1]. Model as factory-shed lots on the outskirts.
- **Plazas and shophouses `[inf]`:** 2-4 storey flat-roof shopfronts with stacked fascia boards, burglar-proof grills, rooftop plastic water tanks; named in the same Nigeria-wide pattern as Anambra and Kaduna.
- **Open gutters `[1]`:** a Commons file is titled "Clearing blocked gutter in Ilorin" [S10]; the Asa channelisation was reported too narrow [S32]. Draw an open concrete drain strip at the kerb edge.

### 3d. Towns on the road out
- **Offa `[2]`:** market town; the Owode market opens every five days and was rebuilt [S20]; Commons shows an evening market and wheelbarrows of dried pepper [S11]; railway station since the colonial period [S20]; indigo dyeing and aso-oke weaving [S20]. Palace is "rebuilt in a modern style" [S20] (scenery only).
- **Omu-Aran `[1]`:** at about 536 m on the Ilorin-Kabba road; farming, hunting, basket-making and blacksmithing [S21]. Laterite and granite deposits are listed for the state [S2]. Hill-town edge with red cuts `[inf]`.
- **Jebba `[2]`:** Niger crossing town split between two states; rail truss bridge (1916), road bridge (1970s), hydro dam, paper mill [S19]. It is in a Kwara North LGA flagged for insecurity (section 9); scenery only if used at all.

## 4. Road
- **Surface:** asphalt in the city, busy and mostly good ("generally good", S3), with congestion and scarce parking; several named avenues have wide carriageways (six Commons files for the main person-named avenue, S12). Rural links patched and edged with red-brown soil `[inf]`.
- **Dual carriageways and flyovers `[2]`:** one road in the capital was dualised and a road near the old market reconstructed [S34]; the Tanke flyover is a reinforced-concrete overpass with two service lanes, finished but kept closed for months [S34]; a second "Unity" flyover is reported [S34]. Federal dualisation of the Ilorin-Ogbomoso road and the Ilorin-Jebba expressway has been approved or is under repair [1: search summaries]; heavy-vehicle gridlock after the Jebba expressway failed was reported in 2021 [1: Daily Trust summary].
- **Kerbs, medians, poles, wires, signs: no source.** `[inf]`: low concrete kerbs and painted edge stones on the dual avenues, none on side streets; red-brown shoulders; concrete utility poles with sagging bundled wires; billboards at junctions; green or blue direction signs. A Commons file "Clearing blocked gutter" and a "Crossroads" file suggest open drains and plain crossroads [S10, S12].
- **Roundabouts:** the only Ilorin roundabout named in the searches is a roundabout on the Zango road near the Maraba interstate park, reported as a crash-prone junction (right-of-way confusion) [1: TheCable summary]; **no source confirms a "Post Office Roundabout"** (section 8). A "split diamond underpass" is reported under construction in Wikipedia's Ilorin text [1: S1].
- **Transport and roadside activity `[2+]`:** keke NAPEP (a "more recent addition", S3), okada (accidents common, helmets mandatory, S3), taxis (cheap; **yellow and green Mazda cabs** in S1, S3, S4 `[3 sources]`), MST mass-transit buses from the Post Office area to the university, Offa garage, Tanke, Oyun and Sango [S3], interstate coaches [S4]. Tricycles are barred from the government-house avenue and from 9 pm to 6 am [2: S36].
- **Trades on the kerb `[1 each, Commons names S10, S11]`:** vulcanizers (tyre repair, 8+ files), barbers, roadside shoemakers, generator repairers, pepper grinders, cement sellers, roadside tomato sellers (9 files), roast-corn women, hawkers of ewedu and fried yam/plantain, loaf kiosks.
- **Bridges:** "Ilorin Bridge" is a Commons file with no description [S12]. The Asa crosses the city and floods its bridges (S32); a bridge on one estate road was flagged at risk. **No bridge in Ilorin is safe to name as a venue.** The nearest well-documented bridge is the Jebba rail bridge, 1916, steel truss with concrete arches (S19), in an insecurity-flagged LGA.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- **Zones `[2]`:** Guinean forest-savanna mosaic across most of the state, West Sudanian savanna in the east (S2); Ilorin itself in Guinea savanna, "gently undulating, hills and plains" (S45, S42). Elevation about 287 m for the state, higher on the escarpment edge (Omu-Aran about 536 m, S21; Owu falls area about 590 m, S6).
- **Trees `[partly sourced]`:** shea (Vitellaria paradoxa) seeds studied in Kwara sites [1: search summary]; cashew is the state's export push and Ilorin's processing hub [2: S1, S44]; mango, pawpaw (Commons files "A very green pawpaw tree", S10), plantain (Offa, S11), oil palm [1: S2 lists palm produce], neem [inf], teak and eucalyptus plantations [inf], locust-bean trees [inf, not found for Kwara]. Palms and silk-cotton trees are typical Yoruba town trees [inf].
- **Crops `[2+]`:** yam, cassava, maize, sorghum, millet, rice (Niger floodplain), onions, beans, soya, sesame, cashew, cotton, tobacco, groundnut (S2, S44); sweet potato and maize at Offa (S20); tomatoes and peppers on roadside stalls (S10, S11). Cattle, goats and sheep are kept (S2, S20): **herd imagery is in the avoid list** (section 6).
- **Rock and relief `[2]`:** Sobi Hill is a granite inselberg of two cones and a saddle, about 394 m, the highest natural point in the city (S5, S3, S1); the state's minerals include granite, quartz, marble and laterite (S2); a "crushed rocks" quarry is named in Commons (S11); the NE edge of the Kukuruku escarpment lies behind Owu (S6). Weathered basement under the city is 5-20 m thick `[1: search summary]`. **Soil colour is `[inf]`**: ferruginous/ferralitic soils are named for Kwara cassava lands `[1]`, so red-brown tracks, pale-grey granite and sandy roadside are safe.
- **Water `[2+]`:** the Asa River splits the city east-west and floods (S32); an Asa Dam impoundment with fish farms and water hyacinth (S33); Oyun and Awun rivers cross the interior (S2); the Niger forms the northern boundary and Jebba lake (S2, S19). River colour is not documented; use muddy green-brown `[inf]`.
- **Climate (Aw) `[2+]`:** maxima 33-37 C, March hottest (S1); mean rainfall about 1,000-1,400 mm with the heaviest rain May-October (S1, S2); about 148 rainy days (S2).
- **Light by season:**
  - **Harmattan, mid-Nov to mid-Feb `[2]`:** dust builds from October, peaks Dec-Jan, fades by April (S43); dry cool air and clear skies in one source (S45), dust haze with about 10-12 km visibility in a late-December forecast `[1]`. Pale sun, hazy hills, dull leaves.
  - **Late dry, Feb-Apr `[2]`:** hottest weeks, bare fields, grass fires `[inf]`.
  - **Rains, May-Oct `[2]`:** green land, heavy downpours (S45); steep paths muddy (S26).
- The calendar unlocks Kwara on 2 Nov 2026, so the world should default to **early dry season with a faint harmattan haze** (the same week as Oyo, Borno and Delta).

## 6. Dress
Sources are weak on Ilorin specifically; garment names are consistent, mixing is `[inf]` except for the cloth facts.
- **Men, everyday `[2]`:** buba and sokoto, agbada for formal wear, fila caps, riga or kaftan; T-shirts and jeans for work and youth (S37, search summary of Yoruba dress). Hausa-side: embroidered cap and turban `[inf from the Kano and Kaduna dossiers]`. The buba/sokoto/riga overlap is the visible Yoruba-Hausa mix.
- **Women `[2]`:** iro (wrapper) and buba with gele; ipele shoulder shawl; ankara prints; hijab and headtie as ordinary dress; a shawl for northern-style outfits (S37). Market and pottery women: wrapper, blouse, headtie, dust apron `[inf]`.
- **Cloth `[2]`:** aso-oke/ofi woven in Ilorin; etu (dark indigo with white stripes), sanyan (tan, cream, brown), alaari (deep red/purple) (S37). Indigo-wine-green stripes were favoured for Ilorin brides in the early 20th century `[1: S38]`. Ofi caps are made in Kwara `[1: S16]`.
- **Children `[inf]`:** school uniforms (checked or plain frocks and shorts), flip-flops. Commons names a thatched-roof elementary classroom `[1]`.
- **Working people `[1 each]`:** vulcanizers in oily overalls, barbers in aprons, pottery women in wrappers and headties, tomato and pepper sellers in wrappers, porters with trolleys and head pads, bricklayers.
- **Footwear:** sandals, flip-flops, slippers; shoes are removed on certain sacred sections of Sobi Hill (S5), which is another reason not to portray them.
- **Avoid list (hard):**
  1. Party colours as clothing, caps, wraps, umbrellas, flags, kekes or canopies: **#D4621B, #1FA98F, #3B6FD4, #F5CE2E, #C23FA0, #8257C8**. Keep every fabric swatch distinct from these by hue and saturation; the yellow-and-green taxi livery (S1, S3, S4) is real but must be painted muted (olive-green body with a dull ochre panel), never saturated yellow-green, and never in a trio with white.
  2. **Political-cap looks:** a crowd of identical fila or any solid saturated red, green, blue or white cap; an NPC in a matching agbada, cap, dark glasses and staff or fan ("politician look"); any single-colour cap repeated.
  3. **Traditional-ruler regalia:** beaded crowns, palace attendants, durbar horsemen with regalia, carved palace-gate poles, sunshades over a ruler. Palaces are scenery only and this state's palace is not even used as scenery.
  4. **Religious dress and practice:** clerical collars, choir robes, white-garment church processions, imam or cleric robes, prayer mats and prayer lines, masquerade costumes (masquerades have a documented ban in Ilorin history, S9), charms or shrine objects, and the prayer vigils associated with the summit of Sobi Hill (S5, S14). No domes, minarets or crosses in the skyline, even as far-field scenery.
  5. **Uniformed forces:** the Sobi barracks and a new army battalion in Omu-Aran are real (S1, S31); no military or police uniforms, camouflage, weapons or checkpoints; no kidnap, bandit or burning imagery.
  6. **Herder-coded imagery:** staff-carrying herdsmen, cattle drives, Fulani-coded costume as a crowd element. Goats and sheep at market are fine.
  7. **Ethnic-stereotype imagery:** "tribal" costume, face marks as decoration, any "Hausa vs Yoruba" joke or pairing; do not label who is who.
  8. **Slogans and branding:** the state's government branding and nickname, and any word that reads as an election slogan ("mandate", "change", "renewal", "sai"); real brand names (use invented ones).

## 11. Hazard visual notes
The pack's three hazards are `rocky-ridge`, `switchback` and `go-slow`.
- **rocky-ridge:** a crest of granite on the road edge, rounded boulders rather than sharp rock (the Sobi/inselberg look, S5). Cheap build: 4-6 instanced flattened spheres in `#8E8A84` and `#9A948A` with a darker streak, piled on one verge and spilling a few stones onto the shoulder; a pale scree strip (flat box `#B8B2A2`); a laterite-red cut bank behind (`#A8643F`); a plain triangular rock warning sign. Sensor: a thin box across the lane edge. No quarry blasting, no falling-rock injury, no collapse.
- **switchback:** a hairpin on the escarpment road (the Omu-Aran/Owu edge sits at 536-590 m, S21, S6). Cheap build: a low concrete-post rail (box posts + a thin box rail) on the outer curve, six chevron boards in alternating off-white and dark grey on the outside, a convex mirror on a pole, a laterite cut bank on the inside, cashew or teak tree-tops visible below the lip. Truck climbing slowly and a loaded keke pulling in at a lay-by add life. Never show a vehicle over the edge.
- **go-slow:** the Post Office bus-park choke point (S3, S4, S12): a nose-to-tail row of high-roofed mass-transit buses, muted green-yellow taxis (section 6 caution), keke, and a goods lorry; hawkers with trays between vehicles; vulcanizer tyre piles at the kerb; a hand-painted "Go-slow" board; amber hazard-light blinks. Use a long block of boxes at low speed. No police, no bribes, no crash.

## 12. Design brief
**Reuse and difference.** Base on **`guinea-savanna`** (neem, mango, boulder, acacia, banana, crops, `redbrown-avenue` road, `hausa-guinea` dress profile as a starting shape), borrowing **`farmland-river`** oil palm and yam-barn feel for the southern fringe. Existing lot styles to reuse: `shophouse`, `flatroof-shopfront`, `zinc-bungalow`, `tiled-villa`, `plaza-block`, `market-shed`, `factory-shed` (cashew/flour plants), `colonial-bungalow` (GRA), `thatch-hut` (very sparingly, escarpment hamlets), `works-shed`. Kwara must not read as Kaduna (also guinea-savanna; planned colonial grid and tree-lined avenues) or Kano (mud, pinnacles): its tells are **an unplanned plastered-earth old-city grain, zinc gables with posted verandas, a wall of red-black pots, a granite twin-cone on the horizon, cloth looms and cashew, and a hot haze**.
