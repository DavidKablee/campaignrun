## 3. Built form
Flat-roofed pale compounds and block shops in a flat, hot city at the river confluence; plaza blocks on the newer roads. The old wall is gone and eight gate sites remain; one (Taramniya) is a roundabout with a symbolic gate. Hausa relief ornament as in Kano and Katsina (**thin**; no Sokoto photo or description reachable). Palace and Central Mosque are barred.

## 4. Road
Pale dust-coloured asphalt, sand shoulders, rain-washed edges (**thin**). Ride: the only hard fact is a 2020 night curfew (after 11 pm) on commercial motorcycles **and** tricycles in the metropolis, which implies both run by day. Wikivoyage says mopeds and tricycles both operate. One undated outlet says okada is banned in the capital. **Unresolved**; see the plan, which recommends keeping the current okada.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, light
Flat Sahel-savanna: neem, deleb and date palms, acacia, sparse grass; wide low rivers with irrigated floodplain plots of rice, sorghum, onions, garlic and tomatoes, lifted by shadoof (summary of fire and farm reports; thin on images). Harmattan haze and heat shimmer, late Oct to Feb.

## 6. Dress
Hausa-Fulani as in Kano: long gown (babban riga) or tunic with a cap or turban; women in a zani wrapper, matching blouse and hijab, in darker everyday colours with white and indigo on ceremony days. One informal guide says Sokoto leans to dark daily wear (single, weak source). **No Sokoto-specific street source** — thin. Avoid party colours and political-cap styling.

## 7. Signs and language
Hausa and English; leather and tannery words need a native check.

## 9. Rejected

## 10. Welcome / farewell
