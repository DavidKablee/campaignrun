## 3. Built form

### 3a. Ibadan: the rust-roof sea (the signature)
- **Look** `[2+: S3, S8, S9, S12]`: the city is "the brown city", named for rusted corrugated-iron roofs on one- and two-storey buildings (the majority of the city) laid over reddish-brown earth, on a range of hills. Wikivoyage's own wording is "all the brown roofs everywhere" from the hilltop hall. A poet's line, "a running splash of rust and gold ... like broken china in the sun", is the standard literary image (S3). A Commons file captions a roof as "captured on a story building" (S12). A travel blog warns the image is partly a social-media cliché, so the rust is real but not the whole city `[1]`.
- **Modelling reading** `[inf]`: a dense mass of low-pitched roof planes at slightly different heights, tilts and rust tones (reds, browns, a few paler silver zinc and a few newer coloured sheets), stepping up and down hillsides; tiny dark gaps for lanes; patched with rooftop tanks, a few satellite dishes, chimneys of cooking fires; a few taller flat-roof blocks and the odd tower poking out. Do not make it uniformly orange; the rust spread should be broad and dull (see palette and the party-colour check in section 12).
- **Hills** `[2+: S1, S3, S8]`: central ridge reaches about 275 m with valleys near 150 m; "seven hills" is the tradition but the sprawl has outgrown them, and sources disagree on the seven names (a user-generated list gives Oke Padi/Padre, Ado, Bola, Mapo, a title-named hill, Sapati, Mokola). The highest hill (its name contains a historic title; reviewer to say if that counts as person-named) carries a colonial memorial tower (person-named); Mapo Hill carries the civic hall; Mokola Hill carries a white hotel and a cultural centre. Do not name the seven.
- **Hill-and-valley streets** `[1 + inf]`: access roads that climb steeply, with houses stepping down the slope and retaining walls; one 2026 report describes a steep access road washed out by rain (S43). A Commons file name "The hill road to [the memorial tower]" confirms a road climbs the highest hill (S14).

### 3b. Old Yoruba compounds
- `[2: S17, S18]` Courtyard compound (agbo-ile) of several houses around an open courtyard; rooms open onto a veranda facing inward; the courtyard larger than the rooms; walls of moulded mud from laterite; thatch/raffia roofs historically, with veranda posts of mud or wood. Compounds fed an organic, dense street pattern in the old quarters, and a travel guide says few unbaked-brick structures survive `[1]`.
- `[1: S18]` Colonial contact brought cement block and corrugated sheet ("zinc"); zinc became a symbol of wealth, and some communities kept mud and thatch on purpose as identity. **So the "old compound" of today is mostly a long, low block of rendered or laterite wall under rust corrugated sheet, with a veranda and a yard, not a thatched mud compound.** Keep a very small share of exposed laterite walling `[inf]`.
- Palaces (Ibadan, Oyo, Ogbomoso, Iseyin and others) have large courtyards, carved posts and gardens (S17, S20). **Scenery only and not even depicted here**; the guardrail forbids a venue and the Mapo Hall problem shows how close they sit to civic spaces.

### 3c. Colonial and early-modern landmarks
- **Neoclassical civic hall (1925-29)** on Mapo Hill, reached by wide steps used as seating `[1: S4]`; a Welsh-engineered colonial hall with a neoclassical front. Form detail (columns? tower?) not given.
- **Afro-Brazilian influence** `[2 on the style, 1 for Ibadan]`: stuccoed two-storey houses with arched windows and decorative masonry are documented for Lagos and Yoruba towns (S17, S18); a travel guide links the style to Ibadan `[1]`. Use sparingly `[inf]`.
- **GRA-type quarters** `[1: S18 IFRA summary says Government Reservation Areas entrenched segregation]`: Agodi is the nearest GRA (S9). Colonial-bungalow form `[inf]`: single-storey, deep veranda, hipped corrugated roof, louvred shutters, garden wall.
- **Modernist 1950s-70s** `[2]`: the university's first buildings in "tropical modernist style" (S50); Cocoa House (S5); a 1970s concrete hotel with five floors and 80 rooms (S8, `[1]`); the white hilltop hotel with a curving drive (S8, S26). The glass "Broking House" in Dugbe (S9 `[1]`).

### 3d. Dugbe and the contemporary city
- **Dugbe** `[2+: S1, S9, S3]`: "commercial nerve centre", most banks and shops in the city, an outdoor market; Cocoa House stands here with malls beside it (S5, S9, S28). The Ring Road loops through Dugbe, Mokola and Bodija; named structures are the Cocoa House Interchange and the Mokola flyover (S19, S1). Surroundings: cafes, shops, local markets (S28).
- **Cocoa House form** `[1 + inf]`: tall slab, 26 floors, 105 m, reinforced concrete, search-summary text calls the facade white and brown `[1]`, with a lower podium that once held a department store, cinema, pool and night club (S27), a mosaic roof garden and a museum on top `[1]`. A small circular domed "Cocoadome" building with mosaic cladding is nearby `[1]`. 
- **Mokola** `[2]`: busy bus stop, an overhead bridge linking Agodi GRA to the university side (search summary of a commentary) and the Mokola flyover (S1, S19); a 550 m flyover with service roads and lights per a 2013 commissioning report (search summary, politician-linked; do not depict the ceremony).
- **Newer plazas and terminals** `[2]`: malls (S9), modern bus terminals at Iwo Road, Challenge and Ojoo (S11, S49; politically commissioned, so used only as unnamed shapes).
- **Challenge, Iwo Road, Ring Road, Molete** are road/interchange place names with roundabouts and bus terminals `[1: S11, S14]`.

### 3e. Oyo town, Ogbomoso, Saki edges
- **Oyo town** `[2: S20, search]`: a dual-carriageway town on the A1; main market (Akesan, also called the king's market) sits next to the palace; markets also at Ajegunle, Owode, Saabo; many schools and hotels; old city walls survive at Old Oyo (ruins). The look of the modern town was not described by any fetched source; assume rust-roof compounds and shopfronts `[inf]`.
- **Ogbomoso** `[1: S21]`: town on a plateau at 347 m, walled compounds, traces of the old wall and moat, cashew and mango plantations, many estates and business complexes. Roads called poor.
- **Saki** `[2: S22, S2]`: hilly, a 490 m inselberg over the savanna; teak, cotton, swamp rice, yam flour, aluminium pot makers, a Thursday market; "breadbasket" of the state.
- **Oke-Ogun hills** `[1 each: S24]`: Iseyin = home of aso-oke weaving; Igbeti = "Marble City", ringed by 16 hills, a rock formation said to resemble a mother with a load on her head; Ikere Gorge Dam with rusted idle turbines; Ado-Awaye hill with a hill-top lake. The existing skyline `inselberg-ridge` fits Saki/Igbeti.

### 3f. Industrial, trade and farm built form
- **Cocoa trade** `[2: S3, S5]`: the historical engine of Ibadan's skyline (Cocoa House was financed from agricultural exports). A research institute for cocoa sits in Ibadan (S46). "Cocoa warehouses" in the shipped pack are `[inf]`: no fetched source describes any warehouse. Model as long low sheds with big sliding doors and stacked jute sacks.
- **Cassava processing** `[1: S29]`: cassava-processing outfits stand around Eleyele Lake. Small processing sheds with drying racks `[inf]`.
- **Rail yard** `[1: S11]`: a Commons file shows Cocoa House viewed from the rail yard; the narrow-gauge line is the original Lagos-Kano line (S2). New standard gauge station is at Moniya, outside the city (S9).

## 4. Road
- **Dual carriageways and flyovers** `[2+: S1, S19, S14]`: nearly all major Ibadan roads are dual carriageways with roundabouts and flyovers; the Ring Road circles the centre; the Mokola flyover; Molete interchange and overhead bridge; Iwo Road interchange (the Ring Road's north-west end); Challenge, CRIN and Ring Road roundabouts (Commons names). The A1 from Lagos to Ibadan to Oyo to Ogbomoso to Ilorin is the spine (S2, S20).
- **Surface** `[inf]` asphalt, dark grey, patched; reinforced-concrete pavement is being laid on several federal corridors (S44) so a few pale concrete stretches are plausible for newer sections. Potholes, sand-bagged patches and washed shoulders are reported (S43).
- **Kerbs/median/poles/signs: no fetched source.** `[inf]` Ring Road has "well-kept medians and street lighting" (S19, weak). Use low concrete kerbs on dual roads, none on side streets, concrete power poles with sagging wires, billboards at junctions, green or blue directional signs.
- **Shoulders** `[inf + 1]` red-brown laterite washing across edges in the rains; a Commons file names an "Off [..] Ibadan road".
- **Traffic** `[2+]`: danfo and cabs, minibuses ("korope" per S35) and okada everywhere (S1, S9, S35, S42); congestion at Dugbe, Mokola and Challenge, with roadside trading blamed (S43). Okada are not banned (S42, dated). Ibadan taxi colour is unknown: Commons has a file named "colorful taxi" (S14), so assume a mix and keep to neutral shades.
- **Roadside activity** `[inf]`: pure-water sellers, roasted-corn and plantain vendors at junctions, cocoa/kola/palm-oil sellers on farm links, cassava sellers; bus-stop clusters at Dugbe, Mokola, Challenge, Iwo Road (S11).
- **Kidnapping and crash locations are not to be dramatised**: the Lagos-Ibadan Expressway, Ijebu-Ibadan road, Igbeti-Igboho axis and Lanlate-Eruwa road are named kidnapping hotspots in S39; a brake-failure crash on the Challenge-Molete road is in a news item. Use these roads as generic dual carriageways only.

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
- **Climate** `[2+: S1, S2, S9]`: tropical wet-and-dry (Aw); rain Mar/Apr-Oct with an August lull; harmattan Nov-Feb; mean ~26 C; humidity ~75-83%; Saki average rainfall ~1,150 mm. Rainy season is greener and cloudier; harmattan brings a pale dust haze `[2: S3, S1]`.
- **Zone** `[2: S1, S45]`: Ibadan "lies completely within the tropical forest zone", near the forest/derived-savanna edge; IITA's forest is secondary lowland forest with emergent Ceiba, Milicia (iroko) and Terminalia, bamboo clumps, raffia along streams, scattered oil palms (S45). North of Oyo the land turns to savanna with inselbergs (S22, S23). So: **more large trees than Kaduna, red earth, hills; fewer open plains until the north.**
- **Crops** `[2+: S2, S22, S46]`: cassava (the dominant crop), maize, yam, cocoa, kola, oil palm, plantain, cashew and mango (Ogbomoso plantations `[1: S21]`), tobacco (Saki), cotton, swamp rice, shea, okra, sorghum, beans. Cattle ranches at Saki and Ibadan (S2) - avoid herding imagery.
- **Planted trees** `[1: S45]`: teak and gmelina plantation stands at the Gambari reserve east of Ibadan. Neem, mango and flamboyant as street trees `[inf]`.
- **Soil** `[2]`: red-brown laterite (S3 "reddish-brown earth", S8, S17 "laterite soils" for walls).
- **Water** `[2]`: Ona, Ogunpa, Ogbere, Kudeti streams in valleys (S1); Eleyele Lake/dam (S1, S29); Asejire Reservoir (S25, always full, tree-lined banks `[1]`); Ikere Gorge reservoir (S24).
- **Light**: rainy season sky humid, grey-blue cumulus and rain-washed light; harmattan late Dec-Feb pale-gold dust, low sun disc, rust roofs desaturate to brown. The "rust and gold" of the poem is a **low-sun, late-afternoon look** `[1: S3]`.
- **Hazard note on water**: Ibadan roads were flooded in 2011 and an Eleyele overspill flood is on record (S29, S43). Do not draw flood scenes.

## 6. Dress
Sources are strong on garment names (S30, S31), weak on everyday colour and on children/working dress.

- **Men, everyday** `[inf]`: tee shirts, polo shirts, short-sleeve shirts, jeans/trousers, sandals or slippers; okada riders in shirts and jackets with helmets (helmet use unknown, `[inf]`); market men in shirts, vests; farmers in faded shirts, wellington boots or sandals.
- **Men, dressy** `[2+: S30, S31]`: buba (embroidered shirt) with sokoto (trousers: sooro slim, kembe loose), dansiki, agbada (3-piece, ceremonial or smart outing; variants like sapara are lighter), cap options (fila): abeti aja (two ear-shaped folds), gobi (side-dropped), akete (wide brimmed). Aso oke fabrics: etu (deep blue, tartan/stripes), sanyan (beige), alaari (maroon/magenta base), waka (black with red warp stripes).
- **Women, everyday** `[2: S30, S31 + inf]`: iro (wrapper) and buba (blouse), ankara prints, gele (head tie), ipele (shoulder shawl), sandals; market women in wrapper, blouse, headtie, apron, with basins on the head.
- **Women, dressy** `[2]`: komole (long iro-and-buba gown), aso oke gele, lace buba, iborun veil for brides.
- **Cloth and market** `[2+: S3, S32, S9]`: adire = indigo resist cloth (cassava paste, raffia ties and stitching), classic blue base with white pattern, made and sold in Ibadan; Dugbe market sells indigo-dyed cloth (S3); Gbagi and Oje are cloth markets (S9). Iseyin = aso-oke weaving (S24).
- **Children** `[inf]`: school uniforms (plain), play clothes. **No school-uniform crowds** (see avoid list item 5).
- **Footwear** `[2]`: sandals, slippers, canvas shoes; "bata" as a general term (S31).
- **Avoid list (hard)**:
  1. **Party colours**: do not use #D4621B, #1FA98F, #3B6FD4, #F5CE2E, #C23FA0, #8257C8 on clothes, caps, kekes, okada, canopies, flags, or any crowd uniform. Also avoid matching tri-colour combinations (red-white-green, green-white, blue-red-white) and a single saturated colour repeated across a crowd.
  2. **Political cap and costume looks**: the fila (especially abeti aja) in a solid saturated colour on many heads; a matching agbada + fila + staff or fan "politician look"; an elder with a staff in a rally pose. If a fila is shown, make it plain, muted and varied (S31 notes it is aso oke or damask), never in a trio with a robe of the same colour.
  3. **Aso-ebi uniformity**: a crowd in one identical print reads as a rally/party team. Vary prints; no single print on more than ~10% of NPCs.
  4. **Real-person signature looks**: no costume that echoes a particular public figure.
  5. **Insecurity imagery** (2026): no camouflage, no armed or uniformed figures, no masks, no school-attack imagery, no children in school-uniform groups near rural roads, no kidnapping, no checkpoint scenes, no cult/gang imagery, no herders or herd cattle.
  6. **Religious dress and objects**: clerical collars, church-choir robes, mosque forms and worship scenes (outdoor hillside gatherings with singing and bells are in S8; leave them out), masquerade costumes, shrine objects, Ifa trays.
  7. **Palaces and ruler regalia**: beaded crowns, palace guards, drummers at a palace.
  8. **Disaster props**: no smoke columns from market fires, no wreckage from blasts, no flooded streets, no collapsed buildings (see section 11).

## 12. Design brief

### What makes Oyo visibly different from the four built states
Kano = mud-brick, dye pits, baobab. Kaduna = colonial grid, big trees, truss bridge. Rivers = creek and flare stacks. Anambra = painted plazas, mansions, river port. **Oyo = a red-brown hill city whose roofs are the colour: rust corrugated roof planes stepping down hillsides in every direction, a single slim 26-storey tower rising out of them, hill-and-valley dual carriageways with flyovers, greener than the north, cassava and cocoa and oil-palm farms outside, inselbergs beyond.**

### Reuse and new work
- **Reusable world libraries** (packages/content/data/world/): **farmland-river** (Anambra: zinc-bungalow + mansion + flatroof-shopfront lots, mango/oilpalm flora, laterite-wash road and cassava crop) is closest for the residential and farm-belt districts. **guinea-savanna** (Kaduna: colonial-bungalow, tiled-villa, plaza-block, maize crop, boulder, redbrown-avenue road) is closest for Agodi GRA and the contemporary corridor. **creek-delta** brings cassava crop and `waterEdge` for the reservoir. **sahel-savanna** is not useful here. No `hills-plateau` library exists, so a **new world library (suggested id `forest-hills`)** is needed, composed mainly of existing lot styles with new road texture and the new styles below.
- **Reusable lot styles** in apps/game/src/render/world/lots.ts: `zinc-bungalow` (the rust-roof block house; the main style), `flatroof-shopfront`, `market-shed`, `market-block` (Dugbe lanes), `plaza-block` (contemporary corridor), `colonial-bungalow` (Agodi GRA), `mansion` (villa belts), `factory-shed` (recolour to cocoa/cassava sheds), `tiled-villa`, `shophouse`.
