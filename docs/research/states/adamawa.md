## 3. Built form
Jimeta: a grid of block shops, plazas and flat-roofed or zinc-roofed houses in cream and ochre; Yola old town: a hillside of mud and block compounds. Highlands: round clay huts with conical thatch, granite walls, terraced fields (Sukur). **Thin**: no source describes a Jimeta street.

## 4. Road
Wide asphalt trunk roads, a flyover at a junction, bridges linking Yola and Jimeta, laterite and dust shoulders, hill roads with bends towards Mubi and the Mandara (the draft hazards switchback and rocky-ridge fit). Yellow Keke Napep are the usual way to move in Jimeta and Yola (Kupi guide, a resident's account on Steemit), motorcycles were banned and minibuses and shuttle buses have largely given way; some sources still list okada and minibuses from parks. No source mentions a hilux fleet, so the ride moves from hilux to keke (see the plan, section 6).

## 5. Vegetation, terrain, water, light and season
Savanna with scattered trees (neem, baobab, acacia); highlands of bare granite and grass; the wide Benue with sandbars; Kiri's reservoir; cotton and millet fields; very hot March–April, harmattan haze December–February; flood risk in September.

## 6. Dress
Fulani: long kaftan or gown, embroidered cap or turban, women in bright wrappers, beads and bangles (blog-grade). Hausa-style babban riga and cap for men; women in hijab, abaya or wrapper and blouse. Avoid: a Lamido's turban and robes, any chief's regalia, red or green party hues in full outfits.

## 7. Signs and language
English and Hausa (lingua franca), Fulfulde, Bachama, Kilba and others. Board words: MARKET, GRAIN, COTTON, ONIONS, POS in English; Hausa words (KASUWA for market) and any Fulfulde need a native check. "CATTLE" is avoided.
