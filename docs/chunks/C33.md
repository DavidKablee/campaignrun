# C33 — Make the characters and the city read as 3D
Status: done      Depends on: C21 (chibi rig), C30 (weather), C32 (portrait framing)
## Goal
"Alive" = closer to 3D and cleaner. C32 made the characters *visible*; they still read as flat
paper dolls — a stack of same-toned cylinders with no shadow, no shoulders and no arms, and the
city behind them was flat colour fills. Fix the reasons things look flat, not the framing.
## Done when
`pnpm verify` green; at studio zoom a character has a readable silhouette (head separate from
shoulders, arms clear of the torso), a lit side and a shadow side, and a contact shadow on the
ground. No regression to the gameplay renderer's draw-call budget.
## Shipped
### Lighting — the single reason everything looked flat
`MeshToonMaterial` puts a directional light's `dot(N, L)` through a 5-step ramp, but hemisphere
light is **not** ramped: its value is a hard floor under every surface in the scene. Both rigs ran
a floor high enough that the floor alone already saturated the ramp's top step, so nothing could
have a shadow side and every object resolved to one tone per colour.

- Studio / portraits: hemisphere 1.25 → 0.42, key 2.0 → 1.0, rim 1.1 → 0.55, plus a front fill at
  0.34. The fill carries more weight than a bounce light normally would, because the face is a
  flat plate with one normal: under the key alone it landed mid-ramp and read as a dark smudge.
- `world.ts`: hemisphere 1.35 → 0.58, sun 2.2 → 1.3. Night is deliberately **not** cut as far
  (0.3 → 0.22, 0.12 → 0.1) — at night the floor is all that keeps the road readable, and NEPA
  multiplies it by another 0.45 on top. All four now live in named constants; the day and night
  values were previously duplicated as literals in the constructor and in `applyWeather`, free to
  drift apart.

### Real shadows on the character screens
`renderer.shadowMap` was never enabled anywhere; gameplay fakes it with `blobShadow` and the
studio/portraits had no ground contact at all, so the character floated. The studio and the
portrait baker now cast from the key light onto the pedestal (studio) or an invisible
`ShadowMaterial` catcher (portraits). The outline shells are excluded from the shadow pass — they
are BackSide copies of the same geometry and would thicken and offset every shadow. The key light
was also moved steeper (2.6, 4.2, 3.0) → (1.7, 5.4, 2.3): at the old angle the shadow was a long
streak that left the pedestal entirely and hung in mid-air.

Gameplay keeps its blob. A shadow map over the whole city is a real cost on the target device and
`patchBend`'s vertex displacement is not applied to the depth material, so world shadows would
misalign with the road curve. Logged in BACKLOG.

### Silhouette
- **Arms.** The pivot sat at `0.155 * sw` against a `0.165` chest — the whole upper arm was inside
  the torso, so in any top whose sleeves share the body colour the character had *no arms*. Pivot
  is now `ARM_X = 0.188`, outboard of `SHOULDER_R`. The stand and run poses also splayed both
  hands *inward* (`rz` +0.1 on the left arm moves the hand toward the body, not away); the signs
  are flipped and the splay widened to 0.2.
- **Torso.** Every chest-level radius is now expressed against `CHEST_R` / `SHOULDER_R`, and the
  whole upper body narrowed (`hi-vis` alone had been 0.172, wider than the arms). A flattened
  ellipsoid shoulder yoke replaces the hard cylinder disc that used to end the torso at chin
  height. Depth went 0.75 → 0.86 (`TORSO_Z`), with outer layers at `LAYER_Z` so they stay proud of
  the chest in depth as well as radius — at the narrower width the old squash made the body a
  board in profile, which the turntable shows every few seconds.
- **Head.** `HEAD_Y` from `SHOULDER_Y - 0.03` to `+0.03`, and the neck nub is taller and shaded
  down. The cranium was sunk into the chest up to the chin.
- **Headwear.** All three newly-exercised pieces were much wider than the head they sit on, which
  read as a lampshade and shaded the face: headtie wrap 0.149 → 0.122 × HS; hardhat shell 0.16 →
  0.133 and its brim 0.175 → 0.146; sailor cap 0.14/0.155 → 0.128/0.142. The gele fan was two wide
  slabs laid flat across the crown (a pagoda brim); it is now three folds, each taller than it is
  wide, leaning back, bases overlapping so it reads as one pleated mass.

### Cleaner
- `HI_SEG = 1.4` on the player / studio / portrait rig: a 12-segment cylinder is visibly a
  dodecagon at studio zoom. 5,730 → 9,298 triangles per hi character (+62 %), **one** of which is
  on screen in gameplay, against a scene measured at 35–64k. Draw calls unchanged (21 meshes
  either way). Crowds and statics stay on `LO_SEG` at 874 triangles, untouched.
- Sleeves are shaded one step off the top colour so the arm keeps an edge where it overlaps.
- `.cr-roster-hero-art` sizes from `aspect-ratio: 5/4` instead of a percentage grid row, and the
  card goes side-by-side between 620 and 900px. Stacked on a full-bleed card the art box was a 4:1
  letterbox with the character stranded thumbnail-sized in the middle of it.
- The studio's two-column breakpoint dropped from 820px to 720px in landscape, for the same reason.
- Portraits render at `setPixelRatio(2)` and supersample down.

### One real bug found while verifying
The picker intermittently fell back to initial letters. `renderPortraits` created **and destroyed
a WebGLRenderer per call**, and the picker calls it twice in the same tick (chips, then heroes) on
top of whatever the splash and game already hold. Browsers cap live contexts (commonly 16) and
drop the oldest; when that happened the bake silently produced nothing — the exact "blank cards"
symptom C32 set out to fix, with a different cause. There is now one long-lived shared offscreen
context, resized per call. Also `PCFSoftShadowMap` is gone in three 0.186 and was warning on every
init; the type is set to `PCFShadowMap` explicitly.

### Test
`apps/game/test/silhouette.test.ts` asserts the three invariants that were actually violated: arms
outboard of the shoulders, no top wider than the arm line, shoulders between chest and head width.
They are plain numbers, so they need no WebGL. 102 tests total (was 99).
## Open
- **Browser-pane only, no real device.** Same debt C20–C32 already carries. Checked at 1280×860
  and 800×620; the phone-width single-column picker and studio were not exercised, and the
  portrait bake cost (12 renders on entering the picker, now at 2x pixel ratio) is unmeasured on a
  low-end Android — which is the device class the budget targets.
- `widestTopR` in `RIG` is maintained by hand. The radii it summarises are literals scattered
  through a `switch`, so the test guards against a *drift* in the invariant, not against someone
  adding a wide top and forgetting to update the constant.
- No shadows in the gameplay world (see above). The blob under the player is still a blob.
- The gele is a cap plus three slabs. Better than a pagoda, still not a sculpted gele.
- `StudioScene`'s camera still uses a literal `0.92` eye height — the portrait camera measures the
  rig, this one does not. It is correct today; it is the same shape of bug C32 fixed.
- Figure/ground contrast on Uncle Boombox and Madam Metronome (carried over from C32) is improved
  by the new lighting but not deliberately addressed.
