# C32 — Contender portraits actually render
Status: done      Depends on: C21 (chibi characters), C31 (roster)
## Goal
"Bring the graphics to life." The Contender picker — the first screen after the splash — showed six flat palette gradients with no character in them. Find out why and fix it, then check whether the rest of the game has the same problem.
## Done when
`pnpm verify` green; every Contender renders a visible face and their signature prop on the picker; nothing overlaps or crops.
## Shipped
Three separate bugs, all pre-existing, stacked on top of each other:

- **Portrait camera aimed above the head.** `renderPortraits` in `render/studio.ts` positioned the camera at `y = 1.58 * height` looking at `1.46 * height` — eye heights from the pre-chibi character. C21 rebuilt the rig at ~4.3 heads tall with `HEAD_Y = 0.93`, so the camera was looking roughly half a world-unit above the crown and the render came back empty but for the top of the head grazing the bottom edge. The `StudioScene` camera right next to it *was* updated at the time (`lookAt(0, 0.92…)`); this one was missed. Now the framing is derived from `Box3.setFromObject(rig.group)` — it measures the rig instead of assuming its proportions, so the next time they change, portraits follow.
- **`headtie` headwear hid the whole face.** `character.ts` built it from a full `b.sphere` (r = 0.18·HS ≈ 0.21, front reach z ≈ 0.23) while the face plate sits at `z = HEAD_R * 1.06 ≈ 0.143` — the sphere enclosed it and depth-occluded it completely, so Mama Ankara rendered as a featureless blob. Every other headwear uses a partial `b.cap`; headtie now does too, with the wrap band lifted from `y: 0` to `y: 0.1·HS` so it crosses the upper forehead instead of the eyes. The gele fold slabs moved from `z: 0.09` (jutting over the brow as a point) to sitting on top of the wrap. This was never visible before because no Contender in the shipped roster used headwear at all — C31 is the first to use `headtie`, `hardhat`, `sailor` and `headphones`.
- **Hero slot cropped to the torso.** `.cr-roster-hero-art img` had `width/height: 100%` inside a grid row of indefinite height, so the percentage fell back to the image's natural 420 px, overflowed the 265 px row and centre-cropped to the midriff. The image is now positioned against a `position: relative` box that fills its row (`height: 100%; min-height: 0`); an earlier attempt with `min-height` overflowed the row by 63 px and bled under the 88 %-opaque copy panel instead.

Supporting changes:
- `renderPortraits` takes `{width, height, frame, yBias}` instead of a single `size`. The picker now bakes two sets: square head-and-shoulders chips for the grid, and a 320×420 half-body hero that includes the signature prop. Note `yBias: 0.5` puts the frame's top edge exactly on the crown (no headroom) regardless of `frame` — the hero uses 0.44.
- Hero row grew from `minmax(180px, 42%)` to `minmax(200px, 52%)` now that it holds a whole character.
- `.claude/launch.json` gained a `game-alt` entry on port 5174: port 5173 was occupied by an unrelated project's dev server, and the existing entry is left untouched.
## Open
- **Browser-pane only, no real device.** Same gap the C20–C30 track already owes: everything here was checked in the desktop browser pane at 1024×768. The picker has not been seen on a phone, and the hero/chip layout at narrow widths (the single-column `.cr-roster-layout` branch) was not exercised.
- Two heroes read weakly against their own palette gradient: Uncle Boombox's torso goes dark against the orange backdrop, and Madam Metronome is a dark figure on light purple. Legible, but the figure/ground contrast is the worst of the six. Worth a palette pass if someone is tuning art anyway.
- The headtie is fixed, not beautiful — the band plus cap still boxes the face in somewhat at studio zoom. A proper gele is a sculptural shape and deserves real modelling rather than a cap and two slabs.
- Portrait baking does 12 renders (6 chips + 6 heroes) in two WebGL contexts on entering the picker. Fine on a desktop; unmeasured on a low-end Android, which is exactly the device class the budget targets.
- The game world itself needed nothing: Lagos renders with stalls, pedestrians, bunting, overhead signage and a working "BRAKE TO LOAD" pickup zone. The defect was confined to baked portraits.
