# C45 — the road decides which ride is right
Status: done      Depends on: C26, C34, C44
## Goal
Make a state's hazards mean something. Thirty-eight packs listed three hazards each and nothing read
them, so every road in the game was generated from one obstacle table and the only reason to change
ride was the seat count.
## Done when
A state's hazards reweight its road and change how the run drives, the briefing says which hazards
are out and what they mean for the ride you picked, and Contender parity still holds on the
harshest roads that ship.

## What was wrong

**Hazards were three words that nothing read.** C34 closed them to an enum of twenty precisely
because they were "the shortlist of road events the game is meant to grow" — and then nothing grew.
`stateSchema.hazards` was loaded, validated and dropped: no sim code, no renderer code, no UI copy
mentioned a single one. Bayelsa listed `flood-sheet` and Borno listed `dust-storm`, and both
produced the same road from the same weighted table.

**So C44's work had nowhere to land.** C44 gave each ride handling of its own and a cost to pay for
it, which only matters if the road ever asks a different question. It never did: one road, one
ranking, and the ranking was the seat count all the way up.

**And C26's size rules were nearly dead content.** A flood patch blocks size L, a narrow gate passes
only size S, a light barrier is ploughed by a Danfo or a Grand Convoy — three real rules, each
firing on about 5% of spawns everywhere, equally, forever. The rides had answers to questions the
road was not asking.

## Shipped

**Hazard effects, as permille of numbers the sim already has.** `packages/content/data/hazards.json`
gives each of the twenty hazards a label, a one-line note, a set of `obstacleWeights` (multipliers on
the spawn weight of one obstacle kind) and a `road` block (top speed, pickup, braking, lane change,
spawn gap, double-spawn chance). Nothing here is a new rule: a flood state is a state where
`flood-patch` is four times as likely, and C26 already decides what a flood patch does to a bus.

**`roadProfile(hazards)` composes them.** One state's hazards multiply into a single profile, which
is the only thing the sim learns about a state — it never sees a hazard name, so adding a hazard is
a content change. The list is sorted into the schema's own order first: permille multiplication
truncates, so a pack author reordering a list must not change how the state plays.

**The clamps in `road.json` are load-bearing.** Three hazards compound, and the failure they compound
towards is a road that multiplies a slow ride below the coast speed — where the speed clamp pins it
and the gas pedal stops meaning anything. The floor is 900 permille on top speed, and a content test
walks all 38 packs × 7 rides to prove no shipped road reaches it.

**Go-slow is deliberately mild.** It is in 37 of the 38 packs, so it is the common denominator of a
Nigerian road rather than a difficulty setting; a test holds every one of its road numbers within 5%
of neutral. What distinguishes one state from another is the other two hazards.

**The briefing says it out loud.** The hazards are named with their notes, and under the ride picker
a verdict on the ride you have chosen: floodwater stops a ride this big, tight gaps suit this one,
a broken surface and this ride hops the holes with room to spare. Every line is read off the
*composed profile* — not written per state — so the briefing cannot promise something the course
generator will not do. Bad news leads, one slot is held for good news when there is any, and three
lines is the limit.

**The baseline says nothing.** Every handling figure is measured against the keke, so on a road whose
hazards are purely handling ones the keke has no opinion at all. A yardstick that praised itself
would make the whole line worthless.

**The knock rule is the counterweight.** Five of the six advice rules read a handling stat, and C44
gave the big rides worse handling across the board — on their own they would advise a Trek on all 38
roads. What a big ride is actually for is surviving: it scatters less of its crowd per knock, and the
Hilux eats one outright. A packed road, or one that hides what is coming, is where that pays.

## Checked

- 287 tests (was 257), `pnpm lint` and `tsc --noEmit` clean, `pnpm test:e2e` 5 passed / 1 skipped.
- **Guardrail 5 re-checked on three real roads** (Adamawa's bends, Borno's dust, Bayelsa's flood).
  C45 lets a state multiply top speed, pickup, braking and lane change for a whole run and two of the
  six Contenders trade on exactly those stats, so parity could not stay a neutral-road result. Still
  within ±3% mean Crowd on all three.
- Momentum re-checked on every ride on every kind of road. C44 found it had never worked because its
  threshold was measured against a speed the clamp made unreachable; the road multiplies that ceiling
  again, so the same cliff was one bad number away.
- Chrome at 390×844, 414×896 and 1280×800: no horizontal overflow at any width, every new text style
  ≥ 9.8:1 against its painted backdrop, the briefing scrolls and reaches the Start button, the
  verdict changes when you change ride (Okada all good → Grand Convoy all bad on Lagos) and when you
  change state.

## Consequences

- **The plausibility bound moved.** `maxCrowdFor` bounds flat rival Crowd by how many entities fit on
  a course at the tightest legal spacing, and a busy road now packs them tighter than it assumed. The
  tightest spacing is `spawn.gapMinMm × course.roadGapFloorPermille`, and a test ties `road.json`'s
  gap clamp to that floor. Without it the bound would have started rejecting honest runs through
  Cross River.
- **Ghost replay takes the road.** A ghost is stored and replayed per state, so the caller passes the
  current state's road; replaying a flood-state ghost on a neutral road would drift it ahead of where
  it really finished.
- Scores are still comparable across this line — nothing here touches a Crowd multiplier — but a run
  through a flooded state is harder than the same run through Abia, which it was not before.

## Open

- **The renderer does not know.** Dust, fog and rain are hazard names the world does not draw; C30's
  weather system is the obvious place to join them, and `low-visibility` currently buys its effect
  entirely in doubled spawns.
- **No hazard is time-of-day or weather aware.** A flash flood is a constant property of a state
  rather than something that happens during a run.
- The numbers were tuned by reading the sim and sweeping all 38 packs, not by playing on a phone.
- Still nothing reads `scenery`, and `priceNgn` is still unused.
